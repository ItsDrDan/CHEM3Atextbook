## New sections proposed

The COR 12 items (types, penetrating/ionizing, whole-number half-life, radiocarbon, fission, fusion, medicine) all have full sections already. No new page is needed. One new H2 subsection groups the "why decay happens" material he teaches before any equation:

| Proposed title | Placement | Content outline | Source | COR item | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nuclear Stability: Why Some Nuclei Decay (H2 subsection) | 14.1, after "Radioactivity and Radioisotopes" (or as the opening H2 of 14.2) | • Proton repulsion vs. the nuclear force; Lego-sculpture and juggling-apples analogies<br>• Neutron-to-proton ratio, **qualitative only** ("not something you have to calculate"): about 1 : 1 for small nuclei (He-4, Li-6, C-12), drifting toward about 1.5 : 1 for heavy ones; a band-of-stability figure<br>• Too many neutrons → beta decay; too few → positron emission/EC; very heavy → alpha<br>• Decay continues parent → daughter → … until a stable nucleus forms (Rn from U-238; "the goal is stability")<br>• Chemical vs. nuclear reaction table (from the Deepen row above) | g--JowvBqM8 @ 3:14–9:12; WDBiyaeivFY @ 0:51–2:28, 10:34–10:59; bZHo3kHvGi0 @ 14:42–14:59; slide 2 | 12.1 (types of radioactivity) | Med |

**Decision for Dr. Dan (scope flag, High):** in bZHo3kHvGi0 @ 30:27 he says he will "never ask you in my class to balance" fission and fusion equations; students only need to tell the two apart. 14.5 Examples 1–2 and the 14.9/ADAPT fission items do ask students to balance them. Either keep balancing (it uses the same top/bottom rule) or mark it as optional/"Going further" and keep the main path to classifying fission vs. fusion.

**Reconcile (Low):** slide 2 and WDBiyaeivFY @ 0:51 say radioactive nuclei are "generally atomic number 20 and higher." The book says "every isotope beyond bismuth (83) is radioactive." Both can be right if written as "instability becomes common above about Z = 20, where nuclei need more neutrons than protons; above Z = 83 every isotope is radioactive."

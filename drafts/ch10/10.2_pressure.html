Learning Objectives

  • Define pressure and explain atmospheric pressure.
  • Describe how a barometer measures air pressure.
  • Convert among atm, mmHg, torr, Pa, kPa, and psi.

You are under pressure right now: the air above you pushes on every square inch of your body with a force of about 14.7 pounds. You do not feel it because the fluids in your body push back just as hard. Pressure is one of the four variables that describe a gas, and health care measures it constantly, from blood pressure to oxygen tanks.

What Is Pressure?

Pressure is force per unit area:

\( P = \dfrac{F}{A} \)

A gas exerts pressure because its particles constantly hit the walls of their container (Section 10.1). The same force spread over a smaller area gives a higher pressure, which is why a needle's sharp point pierces skin easily.

Atmospheric pressure is the pressure exerted by the weight of the air above us. It is about 1 atmosphere at sea level and decreases as you go higher, because there is less air above you.

Measuring Air Pressure: The Barometer

A barometer measures atmospheric pressure. In a mercury barometer, a long glass tube closed at one end is filled with mercury and turned upside down in a dish of mercury. Some mercury flows out, leaving a vacuum at the top, until the weight of the mercury column is balanced by the air pushing down on the dish. At sea level the column is 760 mm tall. That is the origin of the unit millimeters of mercury (mmHg).

Units of Pressure

The table above lists the equalities you will use as conversion factors. The relationships 1 atm = 760 mmHg = 760 torr and 1 atm = 101,325 Pa are exact; 14.7 psi is rounded to 3 significant figures.

Blood pressure is the most common pressure measurement in health care, and it is still reported in mmHg, the unit of the first mercury instruments.

Example \(\PageIndex{1}\): A Fresno barometer reading

Problem. A barometer in a Fresno classroom reads 752 mmHg. Express this pressure in atm and in kPa.

Solution.

\( {752\;\text{mmHg}} \times \dfrac{1\;\text{atm}}{760\;\text{mmHg}} = {0.989\;\text{atm}} \qquad {0.9895\;\text{atm}} \times \dfrac{101.325\;\text{kPa}}{1\;\text{atm}} = {100.\;\text{kPa}} \)

Check. Fresno is only about 300 ft above sea level, so the pressure is just under 1 atm. The decimal point in "100." shows three significant figures.

Example \(\PageIndex{2}\): An oxygen cylinder

Problem. The oxygen in a full portable cylinder is at a pressure of about 2.00 × 103 psi. What is this pressure in atmospheres?

Solution.

\( {2.00 \times 10^{3}\;\text{psi}} \times \dfrac{1\;\text{atm}}{14.7\;\text{psi}} = {136\;\text{atm}} \)

Check. The oxygen is squeezed to more than 100 times atmospheric pressure, which is why cylinders are made of thick steel or aluminum and must be secured so they cannot fall.

Professor Tip

Set up every pressure conversion as a conversion factor with "1 atm" on one side, just like the unit conversions in Chapter 1. When a gas law problem gives two pressures in different units, convert them to the same unit first. When you will use R = 0.082058 L·atm/(mol·K) (Section 10.4), convert pressure to atm.

Clinical Connection: Respiratory Therapy

Respiratory therapists read pressures in several units in one shift: oxygen cylinder gauges in psi, arterial blood gases in mmHg (a normal arterial PO2 is roughly 80 to 100 mmHg), and ventilator airway pressures in centimeters of water (cm H2O). Going further (not on CHEM 3A exams): most of these are gauge pressures, measured relative to the surrounding air: a cylinder gauge reads 0 psi when the cylinder is "empty," even though the gas left inside is still at 1 atm. A blood pressure of 118/76 mmHg likewise means 118 mmHg above atmospheric pressure. (Context only; follow the device manuals and orders.)

Valley Connection: From the Valley Floor to the High Sierra

Fresno sits about 300 ft above sea level, where air pressure averages just under 1 atm. Drive about two hours northeast to Huntington Lake, at about 7,000 ft, and the air pressure drops to roughly 0.77 atm, about 590 mmHg, because a quarter of the atmosphere is now below you. Each breath there holds about a quarter fewer oxygen molecules, which is why visitors from the Valley may feel short of breath on the trail. The lower pressure also lowers water's boiling point (Chapter 9): at Huntington Lake water boils at about 93 °C, so pasta and beans take longer to cook.

Sources: NOAA, NASA, and U.S. Air Force, U.S. Standard Atmosphere, 1976 (pressure versus altitude); U.S. Geological Survey, Geographic Names Information System (elevations).

Common Mistakes

  • Flipping the conversion factor: to go from mmHg to atm, divide by 760.
  • Confusing Pa and kPa: 1 atm = 101,325 Pa = 101.325 kPa.
  • Thinking a higher altitude means higher pressure: there is less air above you, so pressure is lower.

Summary

  • Pressure is force per area; gas pressure comes from particle collisions.
  • Atmospheric pressure is about 1 atm at sea level and drops with altitude; a barometer measures it.
  • 1 atm = 760 mmHg = 760 torr = 101.325 kPa = 14.7 psi.

Exercises

Exercise \(\PageIndex{1}\)

Convert 1.25 atm to mmHg.

Hint

Use 1 atm = 760 mmHg as a conversion factor, with atm on the bottom so it cancels. Keep three significant figures.

Answer

1.25 × 760 = 950. mmHg (written with a decimal point to show 3 significant figures).

Exercise \(\PageIndex{2}\)

A weather report gives the air pressure as 98.5 kPa. What is this in atm and in mmHg?

Hint

Go through atm: divide by 101.325 kPa per atm first, then multiply by 760 mmHg per atm.

Answer

98.5 ÷ 101.325 = 0.972 atm; 0.9721 × 760 = 739 mmHg.

Exercise \(\PageIndex{3}\)

A blood pressure reading is 118/76 mmHg. Express the systolic value, 118 mmHg, in atm.

Hint

Set up a conversion factor with 1 atm on top and 760 mmHg on the bottom so mmHg cancels.

Answer

118 ÷ 760 = 0.155 atm.

Since 1 atm = 760 mmHg exactly, dividing by 760 converts mmHg to atm, and the answer keeps the three significant figures of 118 mmHg.

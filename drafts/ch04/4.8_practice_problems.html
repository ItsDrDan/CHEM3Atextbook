These practice problems cover everything in Chapter 4: reading formulas and counting atoms; classifying compounds as ionic, molecular, or acids; writing ionic formulas with swap-drop and polyatomic ions; naming ionic compounds of fixed-charge and variable-charge metals (with Stock names and the older -ous/-ic names) and simple hydrates; naming binary molecular compounds with Greek prefixes; naming binary acids and oxyacids; and choosing the right set of rules for any compound. They are grouped by section, the Mixed Review group mixes ideas from several sections (and from Chapters 2 and 3) the way an exam does, and a final group practices the memory aids and common traps from Sections 4.2–4.5. Keep a periodic table (the yellow sheet) and the list of polyatomic ions (the green sheet) next to you. Try each problem on paper before you open the answer. Every answer shows the reasoning, and for multiple-choice problems it also explains why each wrong choice is wrong, so read the answer even when you got it right.

4.1 Chemical Formulas and Types of Compounds

Problem 4.1

How many oxygen atoms are in one formula unit of calcium phosphate, Ca3(PO4)2, the main mineral in bone?

A. 4
B. 8
C. 12
D. 13

Hint

The subscript after the parentheses multiplies everything inside. How many O are in one phosphate, and how many phosphates are there?

Answer

B. The subscript 2 after the parentheses multiplies everything inside them: 2 phosphate groups × 4 O each = 8 O atoms.

  • A counts only one phosphate group; it forgets to multiply by the 2 outside the parentheses.
  • C multiplies the 4 by the 3 that belongs to calcium. A subscript only counts the atom (or parentheses) just before it.
  • D is the total number of atoms of all three elements: 3 Ca + 2 P + 8 O = 13. The question asks only for oxygen.

Problem 4.2

Count the atoms of each element, and the total number of atoms, in (a) Mg(OH)2, the active ingredient in milk of magnesia; (b) (NH4)2SO4, a nitrogen fertilizer; (c) Ca(C2H3O2)2, calcium acetate; (d) 4 H2O2, four molecules of hydrogen peroxide.

Hint

List each element with its count. A subscript outside parentheses multiplies only what is inside; a coefficient in front multiplies the whole formula.

Answer
  • (a) Mg: 1. O: 1 × 2 = 2. H: 1 × 2 = 2. Total 5 atoms.
  • (b) N: 1 × 2 = 2. H: 4 × 2 = 8. S: 1. O: 4. Total 15 atoms. The S and O are outside the parentheses, so they are not multiplied.
  • (c) Ca: 1. C: 2 × 2 = 4. H: 3 × 2 = 6. O: 2 × 2 = 4. Total 15 atoms.
  • (d) The coefficient 4 multiplies the whole formula: H: 2 × 4 = 8. O: 2 × 4 = 8. Total 16 atoms in four molecules.

Problem 4.3

Which compound is molecular?

A. NH4Cl
B. N2O
C. KNO3
D. Fe(OH)3

Hint

Check the first part of each formula: a metal or NH4+ means ionic. Which compound is made only of nonmetals, with no ammonium ion?

Answer

B. N2O (nitrous oxide, the "laughing gas" used in dental offices) contains only nonmetals, nitrogen and oxygen, so its atoms share electrons in a molecule.

  • A starts with the ammonium ion, NH4+, so it is ionic even though every element in it is a nonmetal.
  • C starts with potassium, a metal: it is ionic (K+ and NO3−).
  • D starts with iron, a metal: it is ionic (Fe3+ and three OH− ions). Ending in OH does not make it an acid.

Problem 4.5

Which list contains only elements that exist as diatomic molecules when they are uncombined?

A. hydrogen, nitrogen, oxygen, chlorine
B. hydrogen, helium, nitrogen, oxygen
C. sodium, chlorine, bromine, iodine
D. oxygen, sulfur, fluorine, chlorine

Hint

Recall the seven diatomic elements and where they sit on the periodic table (the “7” shape). Check every element in each list; one element off that list rules the list out.

Answer

A. H2, N2, O2, and Cl2 are four of the seven diatomic elements (H2, N2, O2, F2, Cl2, Br2, I2).

  • B includes helium, a noble gas that exists as single atoms.
  • C includes sodium, a metal. Chlorine, bromine, and iodine are diatomic, but sodium is not.
  • D includes sulfur, which is not one of the seven. (It sits below oxygen, but the "7" shape on the periodic table goes N, O, F and then down the halogens.)

Problem 4.6

A student counts the atoms in aluminum carbonate, Al2(CO3)3, and writes "Al: 2, C: 1, O: 3, total 6." (a) What mistake did the student make? (b) Give the correct counts.

Hint

What does the subscript after the parentheses do? Count the atoms in one carbonate group first, then multiply by the number of groups.

Answer

(a) The student ignored the subscript 3 after the parentheses. That 3 means there are three whole carbonate groups.

(b) Al: 2. C: 1 × 3 = 3. O: 3 × 3 = 9. Total 14 atoms.

Problem 4.7

When fluoride from toothpaste or fluoridated water reaches tooth enamel, some of the enamel mineral changes to fluorapatite, Ca5(PO4)3F, which resists acid better. (a) Count the atoms of each element and the total. (b) Is fluorapatite ionic or molecular? Name its three ions. (c) Show that the total charge is zero.

Hint

(a) Multiply only what is inside the parentheses. (b) Look at the first element. (c) Multiply each ion's charge by how many of that ion there are, then add.

Answer

(a) Ca: 5. P: 1 × 3 = 3. O: 4 × 3 = 12. F: 1. Total 21 atoms.

(b) Ionic: it starts with calcium, a metal. The ions are calcium (Ca2+), phosphate (PO43−), and fluoride (F−).

(c) 5(2+) + 3(3−) + 1(1−) = 10+ + 9− + 1− = 0.

Problem 4.8

Ammonium nitrate, NH4NO3, is a nitrogen fertilizer used on Central Valley farms. Which statement about it is true?

A. It is molecular, because every element in it is a nonmetal.
B. It is ionic, made of NH4+ and NO3− ions.
C. It is an acid, because its formula contains hydrogen.
D. One formula unit contains 3 nitrogen atoms.

Hint

Is the first part of the formula a metal, a nonmetal atom, or a polyatomic ion from the green sheet? Count the N atoms in the whole formula before you trust any atom count.

Answer

B. The formula starts with the ammonium ion, NH4+, so the compound is ionic. Its anion is nitrate, NO3−.

  • A falls into the ammonium trap: ammonium is the one cation in this course with no metal, but it is still an ion.
  • C is wrong: acids start with H. Here the H atoms are inside the ammonium ion.
  • D is wrong: there is one N in NH4+ and one in NO3−, so 2 N atoms (with 4 H and 3 O, 9 atoms in all).

4.2 Writing Ionic Formulas

Problem 4.9

Write the formula of the ionic compound formed by each pair of elements: (a) lithium and oxygen; (b) aluminum and sulfur; (c) calcium and nitrogen; (d) potassium and iodine; (e) barium and fluorine.

Hint

Get each ion's charge from its group on the periodic table, swap the charge numbers down as subscripts, reduce, and check that the total charge is zero.

Answer
  • (a) Li+ and O2−: Li2O. Check: 2(1+) + (2−) = 0.
  • (b) Al3+ and S2−: Al2S3. Check: 2(3+) + 3(2−) = 6+ + 6− = 0.
  • (c) Ca2+ and N3−: Ca3N2. Check: 3(2+) + 2(3−) = 0.
  • (d) K+ and I−: KI (equal and opposite charges, 1 : 1).
  • (e) Ba2+ and F−: BaF2. Check: (2+) + 2(1−) = 0.

Problem 4.10

What is the formula of magnesium phosphate?

A. MgPO4
B. Mg3(PO4)2
C. Mg2(PO4)3
D. Mg3P2

Hint

Write the ions first: Mg2+ and phosphate from the green sheet. Swap-drop, then check which choice gives zero total charge and still contains phosphate.

Answer

B. Mg2+ and PO43−: swap-drop gives Mg3(PO4)2. Check: 3(2+) + 2(3−) = 6+ + 6− = 0.

  • A is not neutral: (2+) + (3−) = 1−.
  • C puts each ion's own charge on itself instead of swapping: 2(2+) + 3(3−) = 4+ + 9− = 5−.
  • D is magnesium phosphide, made with the P3− ion, not phosphate. It has no oxygen.

Problem 4.11

Give the formula and charge of each ion: (a) nitrite; (b) perchlorate; (c) hypochlorite; (d) hydrogen phosphate; (e) dihydrogen phosphate; (f) bisulfite; (g) dichromate; (h) acetate.

Hint

Start from the -ate ion on the green sheet. Change the O count for -ite, per-, or hypo- (charge stays the same); each added H+ makes the charge one less negative.

Answer
  • (a) NO2−: one less O than nitrate, NO3−, same charge.
  • (b) ClO4−: one more O than chlorate, ClO3−.
  • (c) ClO−: two fewer O than chlorate (one less than chlorite, ClO2−).
  • (d) HPO42−: adding one H+ to PO43− makes the charge one less negative, from 3− to 2−.
  • (e) H2PO4−: two H+ added, so 3− becomes 1−.
  • (f) HSO3−: "bi-" means one added H+, on sulfite, SO32−.
  • (g) Cr2O72−.
  • (h) C2H3O2− (also written CH3COO−).

Problem 4.13

Use the anions to find the charge on the metal ion in each compound: (a) Cr2(SO4)3; (b) SnO2; (c) Cu2O; (d) Co3(PO4)2; (e) FeO.

Hint

Start from the anion you know. Find the total negative charge, then divide by the number of metal ions to get each metal ion's charge.

Answer
  • (a) 3 SO42− = 6−, shared by 2 Cr: Cr3+.
  • (b) 2 O2− = 4−, on 1 Sn: Sn4+. (The formula was reduced from Sn2O4; the subscript 2 is not tin's charge.)
  • (c) 1 O2− = 2−, shared by 2 Cu: Cu+.
  • (d) 2 PO43− = 6−, shared by 3 Co: Co2+.
  • (e) 1 O2− = 2−, on 1 Fe: Fe2+.

Problem 4.14

Calcium nitrate is sold as a fertilizer that supplies both calcium and nitrogen. What is its formula?

A. CaNO3
B. Ca(NO3)2
C. Ca2NO3
D. CaNO32

Hint

Write Ca2+ and NO3−. How many nitrates balance one calcium, and how do you show more than one polyatomic ion?

Answer

B. Ca2+ needs two NO3− ions, and two of a polyatomic ion go in parentheses: Ca(NO3)2. Check: (2+) + 2(1−) = 0.

  • A is not neutral: (2+) + (1−) = 1+.
  • C has the ratio backward: 2(2+) + (1−) = 3+.
  • D has the right idea but no parentheses, so it reads as 32 oxygen atoms in one ion. Never change or add to the subscripts inside a polyatomic ion.

Problem 4.15

A student writes these formulas. Which are correct? Fix the ones that are wrong. (a) magnesium oxide, MgO2; (b) aluminum hydroxide, Al(OH)3; (c) calcium chloride, CaCl; (d) barium nitrate, Ba(NO3)2; (e) sodium sulfate, NaSO4.

Hint

Write the ions with their charges for each and check whether the total charge is zero. Fix a wrong one by changing how many ions, never the ion itself.

Answer
  • (a) Wrong. Mg2+ and O2− combine 1 : 1: MgO. MgO2 would have 4− of charge against 2+.
  • (b) Correct. (3+) + 3(1−) = 0.
  • (c) Wrong. Ca2+ needs two Cl−: CaCl2.
  • (d) Correct. (2+) + 2(1−) = 0.
  • (e) Wrong. Sulfate is 2−, so it needs two Na+: Na2SO4.

Problem 4.16

A Valley farm supply store sells three fertilizers and soil amendments: potassium nitrate, ammonium sulfate, and calcium sulfate. (a) Write the formula of each. (b) How many nitrogen atoms are in one formula unit of each?

Hint

(a) Find each ion (green sheet for polyatomic ions) and balance the charges. (b) Count the N in each formula; each ammonium ion contains one N.

Answer

(a) Potassium nitrate: K+ and NO3−, KNO3. Ammonium sulfate: two NH4+ for one SO42−, (NH4)2SO4. Calcium sulfate: Ca2+ and SO42−, CaSO4.

(b) KNO3: 1 N. (NH4)2SO4: 2 N (one in each ammonium ion). CaSO4: 0 N; it supplies calcium and sulfur instead.

Problem 4.17

A metal M forms an oxide with the formula M2O3. Assuming the metal keeps the same charge, what is the formula of its chloride?

A. MCl
B. MCl2
C. MCl3
D. M3Cl

Hint

Use the oxide to find the metal's charge: three O2− give how much negative charge, shared by two M? Then pair that cation with Cl−.

Answer

C. In M2O3, three O2− ions give 6−, shared by two M ions, so M is 3+. M3+ needs three Cl−: MCl3.

  • A would need M+.
  • B would need M2+; it reads the subscript 2 in M2O3 as the charge.
  • D has a total charge of 3(3+) + (1−) = 8+, so it is not neutral.

4.3 Naming Ionic Compounds

Problem 4.18

Each of these compounds is found in a hospital, a pharmacy, or a medicine cabinet. Write its formula: (a) potassium chloride; (b) magnesium sulfate; (c) sodium bicarbonate; (d) calcium carbonate (an antacid); (e) zinc oxide (a sunscreen and skin protectant); (f) silver nitrate (on applicator sticks used to stop small areas of bleeding); (g) sodium fluoride.

Hint

Check whether each metal is on the fixed-charge list. Then pair the cation with the anion (green sheet for polyatomic ions) and balance the charges.

Answer

All seven metals are on the fixed-charge list, so the charges come straight from the metal (and none of the names needs a Roman numeral).

  • (a) K+, Cl−: KCl.
  • (b) Mg2+, SO42−: MgSO4.
  • (c) Na+, HCO3−: NaHCO3.
  • (d) Ca2+, CO32−: CaCO3.
  • (e) Zn2+, O2−: ZnO.
  • (f) Ag+, NO3−: AgNO3.
  • (g) Na+, F−: NaF.

Problem 4.19

Give the Stock name (with a Roman numeral) and the older -ous/-ic name of each compound: (a) Fe(OH)3; (b) CuCl; (c) Cr2O3; (d) Pb(NO3)2; (e) Co(NO3)3; (f) SnCl4.

Hint

Find each metal's charge from the anions and write it as a Roman numeral. For the older name, use the Latin root with -ous for the lower charge, -ic for the higher.

Answer
  • (a) 3 OH− = 3−, so Fe3+: iron(III) hydroxide, ferric hydroxide.
  • (b) 1 Cl−, so Cu+: copper(I) chloride, cuprous chloride.
  • (c) 3 O2− = 6− over 2 Cr, so Cr3+: chromium(III) oxide, chromic oxide.
  • (d) 2 NO3− = 2−, so Pb2+: lead(II) nitrate, plumbous nitrate.
  • (e) 3 NO3− = 3−, so Co3+: cobalt(III) nitrate, cobaltic nitrate.
  • (f) 4 Cl− = 4−, so Sn4+: tin(IV) chloride, stannic chloride.

The -ous name always goes with the lower of the metal's two charges and -ic with the higher.

Problem 4.20

What is the name of Fe2(SO4)3?

A. iron(II) sulfate
B. iron(III) sulfate
C. diiron trisulfate
D. iron(III) sulfite

Hint

Start from sulfate's charge on the green sheet. Three sulfates carry how much negative charge, and how many iron ions share it?

Answer

B. Three sulfate ions give 3(2−) = 6−, shared by two iron ions, so each is Fe3+: iron(III) sulfate (ferric sulfate).

  • A uses the subscript 2 as the charge. The Roman numeral is the charge of one iron ion.
  • C uses Greek prefixes, which are for molecular compounds; this is ionic.
  • D changes the anion. SO42− is sulfate; sulfite is SO32−.

Problem 4.21

Which compound needs a Roman numeral (or an -ous/-ic ending) in its name?

A. ZnCl2
B. AgBr
C. SnF2
D. Al2O3

Hint

Which metal is not on the fixed-charge list (group 1, group 2, Al, Zn, Ag)? That metal's name must state its charge.

Answer

C. Tin forms both Sn2+ and Sn4+, so the name must say which: tin(II) fluoride, or stannous fluoride.

  • A: zinc is always Zn2+, so ZnCl2 is simply zinc chloride.
  • B: silver is always Ag+ in this course, so AgBr is silver bromide.
  • D: aluminum is always Al3+, so Al2O3 is aluminum oxide.

Problem 4.23

Each name below has an error. Explain the error and give the correct name. (a) NaCl, "sodium(I) chloride"; (b) FeCl2, "iron chloride"; (c) Cu2S, "copper(II) sulfide"; (d) Mg(OH)2, "magnesium dihydroxide"; (e) CaSO4, "calcium sulfide."

Hint

For each, ask: is the metal fixed-charge or variable? Is the compound ionic (no prefixes)? Does the anion name match the anion's formula?

Answer
  • (a) Sodium is a fixed-charge metal, so no Roman numeral: sodium chloride.
  • (b) Iron has two common charges, so the name must give one. 2 Cl− = 2−, so Fe2+: iron(II) chloride (ferrous chloride).
  • (c) One S2− is shared by two copper ions, so each is Cu+: copper(I) sulfide (cuprous sulfide). The student used the subscript as the charge.
  • (d) Ionic names never use prefixes: magnesium hydroxide.
  • (e) SO42− is the polyatomic ion sulfate, which keeps its own name; sulfide is S2−: calcium sulfate.

Problem 4.24

Two common fluoride sources in toothpaste are stannous fluoride and sodium fluoride. (a) Write the formula of each. (b) Give the Stock name of stannous fluoride. (c) Why does one name need a charge (in the -ous ending or a Roman numeral) but the other does not? (d) What would the formula of stannic fluoride be?

Hint

Stannous is the lower charge of tin. Is sodium on the fixed-charge list? For (d), -ic is the higher charge; swap-drop it with F−.

Answer

(a) Stannous means tin(II), Sn2+, so stannous fluoride is SnF2. Sodium fluoride is NaF.

(b) Tin(II) fluoride.

(c) Tin can form Sn2+ or Sn4+, so the name must say which. Sodium is in group 1 and is always Na+, so "sodium fluoride" can mean only one compound.

(d) Stannic is the higher charge, Sn4+: SnF4, tin(IV) fluoride.

Problem 4.25

Iron supplements are often sold as ferrous sulfate. What is its formula?

A. FeSO4
B. Fe2(SO4)3
C. FeSO3
D. Fe2SO4

Hint

Ferrous means the lower charge of iron. Write that ion with sulfate (check -ate versus -ite on the green sheet) and balance the charges.

Answer

A. Ferrous is the lower charge of iron, Fe2+ (iron(II)). Fe2+ and SO42− combine 1 : 1: FeSO4, iron(II) sulfate.

  • B is ferric sulfate, iron(III) sulfate. The -ic ending is the higher charge, Fe3+.
  • C is iron(II) sulfite. SO32− is sulfite, not sulfate.
  • D is not neutral with Fe2+: 2(2+) + (2−) = 2+. It would need Fe+, which is not an iron ion in this course.

Problem 4.26

Hydrates show up on farms and in the lab. (a) Name CaSO4·2H2O, gypsum, which Valley growers spread to improve soil. (b) Name CoCl2·6H2O, the pink form of the cobalt compound used in some humidity indicator cards. (c) Zinc sulfate heptahydrate is sprayed on some orchards to correct zinc deficiency. Write its formula. (d) How many hydrogen atoms and how many oxygen atoms are in one formula unit of gypsum?

Hint

Name the salt first, then add a Greek prefix plus "hydrate" for the waters. For (d), count H and O in both the CaSO4 part and the waters.

Answer

(a) Calcium sulfate dihydrate (di- = 2 waters).

(b) 2 Cl− = 2−, so Co2+: cobalt(II) chloride hexahydrate (hexa- = 6 waters).

(c) Zn2+ and SO42−, plus hepta- = 7 waters: ZnSO4·7H2O. Zinc is a fixed-charge metal, so there is no Roman numeral in the name.

(d) H: 2 × 2 = 4, all in the water. O: 4 in sulfate + 2 × 1 in the water = 6.

4.4 Naming Binary Molecular Compounds

Problem 4.27

Name each molecular compound: (a) CO; (b) N2O; (c) PCl3; (d) N2O3; (e) SiCl4; (f) BrF5; (g) S2Cl2.

Hint

Both elements are nonmetals, so use prefixes. No mono- on the first element, -ide on the second, and drop the a or o before "oxide."

Answer
  • (a) carbon monoxide (mono- is used on the second element, and "monooxide" drops an o).
  • (b) dinitrogen monoxide, commonly called nitrous oxide.
  • (c) phosphorus trichloride.
  • (d) dinitrogen trioxide.
  • (e) silicon tetrachloride.
  • (f) bromine pentafluoride.
  • (g) disulfur dichloride.

No name starts with mono-, and every second element ends in -ide.

Problem 4.28

Write the formula of each compound: (a) carbon tetrabromide; (b) nitrogen dioxide; (c) xenon tetrafluoride; (d) diboron trioxide; (e) iodine monochloride; (f) tetrasulfur tetranitride.

Hint

Each prefix is a subscript: mono- = 1, di- = 2, tri- = 3, tetra- = 4. There are no charges to balance, and a molecular formula is never reduced.

Answer

(a) CBr4. (b) NO2. (c) XeF4. (d) B2O3. (e) ICl. (f) S4N4. The prefixes give the subscripts directly; there are no charges to balance, and S4N4 is not reduced to SN.

Problem 4.29

What is the name of P4O6?

A. phosphorus oxide
B. tetraphosphorus hexoxide
C. diphosphorus trioxide
D. phosphorus(III) oxide

Hint

Classify first: two nonmetals means Greek prefixes with the exact atom counts. Molecular formulas are never reduced, and Roman numerals are only for metals.

Answer

B. Phosphorus and oxygen are both nonmetals, so the name uses prefixes: tetra- for 4 P and hexa- for 6 O, with the a dropped before "oxide."

  • A leaves out the prefixes, so it does not say which phosphorus oxide this is (P4O10 is also a phosphorus oxide).
  • C is the name of P2O3. Molecular formulas are never reduced, so the name must match P4O6.
  • D uses a Roman numeral, which is only for variable-charge metals in ionic compounds.

Problem 4.30

Which formula and name are correctly matched?

A. CO, monocarbon monoxide
B. SO3, sulfur trioxide
C. PCl5, phosphorus chloride
D. NF3, nitrogen(III) fluoride

Hint

Check three rules for molecular names: no mono- on the first element, a prefix on the second element, and no Roman numerals.

Answer

B. One S (no prefix on the first element) and three O (tri-), with the -ide ending.

  • A uses mono- on the first element. CO is carbon monoxide.
  • C is missing the prefix on chlorine: phosphorus pentachloride. (PCl3 is also a phosphorus chloride.)
  • D uses a Roman numeral on a nonmetal. NF3 is nitrogen trifluoride.

Problem 4.31

Air monitoring stations across the San Joaquin Valley measure several of the U.S. EPA's "criteria" air pollutants, including ozone (O3), NO2, CO, and SO2. (a) Name NO2, CO, and SO2. (b) Which of the four is not a compound? Explain. (c) Nitric oxide, NO, forms in car engines and turns into NO2 in air. Give the systematic name of NO.

Hint

(a) Two nonmetals: use prefixes. (b) A compound needs at least two different elements. (c) Give each element its prefix; mono- is used on the second element.

Answer

(a) NO2: nitrogen dioxide. CO: carbon monoxide. SO2: sulfur dioxide.

(b) Ozone, O3, contains only one element (oxygen), so it is a form of the element, not a compound. A compound must contain at least two different elements.

(c) Nitrogen monoxide.

Problem 4.32

Which compound is molecular and correctly named?

A. AlCl3, aluminum trichloride
B. PCl3, phosphorus trichloride
C. MgCl2, magnesium dichloride
D. NaCl, sodium monochloride

Hint

First find which compounds are molecular (nonmetals only). Ionic compounds never take prefixes.

Answer

B. Phosphorus and chlorine are both nonmetals, so prefixes are used.

  • A is ionic (aluminum is a metal); its name is aluminum chloride, with no prefix.
  • C is ionic; its name is magnesium chloride.
  • D is ionic; its name is sodium chloride.

Problem 4.33

Some molecular compounds go by common names. Valley dairies capture methane from manure as biogas; ammonia is applied as a fertilizer; and 3% hydrogen peroxide is a common disinfectant. (a) Write the formula of each, and of water. (b) How many atoms are in one molecule of each? (c) A student "simplifies" H2O2 to HO. Why is that wrong?

Hint

These are the common names in Section 4.4. For (b), add the subscripts. For (c), is a molecular formula ever reduced?

Answer

(a) Methane, CH4. Ammonia, NH3. Hydrogen peroxide, H2O2. Water, H2O.

(b) CH4: 5 atoms. NH3: 4 atoms. H2O2: 4 atoms. H2O: 3 atoms.

(c) A molecular formula gives the actual atoms in one molecule. A hydrogen peroxide molecule really contains 2 H and 2 O atoms, so its formula is never reduced.

Problem 4.34

A student names N2O4 "nitrogen dioxide," reasoning that 2 : 4 reduces to 1 : 2, and names SiO2 "monosilicon dioxide." Correct both names and explain each mistake.

Hint

Molecular formulas give the real atom counts, so is N2O4 the same molecule as NO2? Then check the rule for mono- on the first element.

Answer

N2O4 is dinitrogen tetroxide (or tetraoxide). Molecular formulas are not reduced: N2O4 and NO2 are different molecules, and "nitrogen dioxide" is the name of NO2.

SiO2 is silicon dioxide. Mono- is never used on the first element.

4.5 Naming Acids and Bases

Problem 4.35

Name each acid: (a) HI(aq); (b) H2SO4; (c) H2SO3; (d) HClO4; (e) H2S(aq); (f) HC2H3O2; (g) HIO4.

Hint

Go through the anion each time: name the ion after the H. -ide gets hydro-...-ic, -ate becomes -ic, -ite becomes -ous, and per- or hypo- stays.

Answer
  • (a) Anion I−, iodide: hydroiodic acid.
  • (b) Anion SO42−, sulfate: sulfuric acid (the root is lengthened to sulfur-).
  • (c) Anion SO32−, sulfite: sulfurous acid.
  • (d) Anion ClO4−, perchlorate: perchloric acid.
  • (e) Anion S2−, sulfide: hydrosulfuric acid.
  • (f) Anion C2H3O2−, acetate: acetic acid, the acid in vinegar.
  • (g) Anion IO4−, periodate (one more O than iodate, IO3−): periodic acid, pronounced "per-iodic."

Only (a) and (e) have no oxygen, so only they get hydro-.

Problem 4.36

Write the formula of each acid: (a) hydrochloric acid; (b) phosphoric acid; (c) carbonic acid; (d) hypobromous acid; (e) oxalic acid; (f) iodic acid.

Hint

Work backward to the anion: hydro-...-ic from -ide, -ic from -ate, -ous from -ite (keep hypo-). Then add one H+ per negative charge.

Answer
  • (a) hydro-...-ic from chloride, Cl−: HCl(aq).
  • (b) -ic from phosphate, PO43−; three H+ cancel 3−: H3PO4.
  • (c) -ic from carbonate, CO32−: H2CO3.
  • (d) hypo-...-ous from hypobromite, BrO−: HBrO.
  • (e) from oxalate, C2O42−: H2C2O4.
  • (f) -ic from iodate, IO3−: HIO3.

Problem 4.37

What is the name of H2SO3?

A. sulfuric acid
B. sulfurous acid
C. hydrosulfurous acid
D. hydrosulfuric acid

Hint

Which anion is SO32−: sulfate or sulfite? Switch its ending, and remember that hydro- is only for acids with no oxygen.

Answer

B. The anion is SO32−, sulfite. An -ite ion becomes an -ous acid: sulfurous acid.

  • A is H2SO4, from sulfate (-ate → -ic).
  • C adds hydro- to an oxyacid. Hydro- is only for acids with no oxygen.
  • D is H2S(aq), the binary acid from sulfide.

Problem 4.38

When bleach dissolves in water, the main germ-killing species is an acid with the formula HClO. Some wound-care solutions also contain it. What is its name?

A. chloric acid
B. chlorous acid
C. hypochlorous acid
D. hydrochloric acid

Hint

Name the anion ClO− first (one less O than chlorite). Keep any prefix, and switch -ite to -ous or -ate to -ic.

Answer

C. The anion ClO− is hypochlorite. The -ite ending becomes -ous, and the prefix hypo- stays: hypochlorous acid.

  • A is HClO3, from chlorate.
  • B is HClO2, from chlorite.
  • D is HCl(aq), from chloride; it has no oxygen.

Problem 4.39

Bromine forms the same family of oxyanions as chlorine. For each anion, give the formula and name of its acid: (a) bromide; (b) bromate; (c) bromite; (d) hypobromite; (e) perbromate.

Hint

Write each anion from the chlorine family pattern (all 1−), add one H+, then name the acid: -ide to hydro-...-ic, -ate to -ic, -ite to -ous.

Answer
  • (a) Br−: HBr(aq), hydrobromic acid.
  • (b) BrO3−: HBrO3, bromic acid.
  • (c) BrO2−: HBrO2, bromous acid.
  • (d) BrO−: HBrO, hypobromous acid.
  • (e) BrO4−: HBrO4, perbromic acid.

All five anions are 1−, so every acid has one H.

Problem 4.40

(a) Vinegar contains acetic acid. Write the formulas of acetic acid and of the acetate ion. Acetic acid has 4 H atoms in all; why is only one of them written in front? (b) Spinach contains oxalic acid, and calcium oxalate is the most common compound in kidney stones. Write the formulas of oxalic acid and calcium oxalate, and count the atoms in one formula unit of calcium oxalate.

Hint

(a) Which H is written in front, and what is the acetate ion's charge? (b) Oxalate is 2− on the green sheet; balance it with H+ or with Ca2+.

Answer

(a) Acetic acid is HC2H3O2 (also written CH3COOH), and acetate is C2H3O2−. Only the H written in front is released as H+; the other 3 H atoms are part of the acetate ion. Acetate has a 1− charge, so the acid has one H+.

(b) Oxalate is C2O42−, so oxalic acid is H2C2O4 and calcium oxalate (Ca2+ with C2O42−) is CaC2O4: Ca 1, C 2, O 4, 7 atoms.

Problem 4.41

Find and fix the error in each acid name: (a) H3PO4, "hydrophosphoric acid"; (b) pure HCl gas, "hydrochloric acid"; (c) HNO2, "nitric acid"; (d) H2CO3, "carbonous acid."

Hint

For each, find the anion and ask: does it contain oxygen (then no hydro-)? Is it -ate (-ic) or -ite (-ous)? Is the compound dissolved in water?

Answer
  • (a) It is an oxyacid, so there is no hydro-: phosphoric acid (from phosphate).
  • (b) Without water it is not an acid solution: hydrogen chloride. Only HCl(aq) is hydrochloric acid.
  • (c) NO2− is nitrite, and -ite becomes -ous: nitrous acid. Nitric acid is HNO3.
  • (d) CO32− is carbonate, and -ate becomes -ic: carbonic acid.

Problem 4.42

An oxyacid has the formula H3XO4 and a name that ends in "-ic acid." What are the charge and the name ending of its anion, XO4?

A. 3−; the anion name ends in -ate
B. 3−; the anion name ends in -ite
C. 3+; the anion name ends in -ate
D. 4−; the anion name ends in -ide

Hint

How many H+ does the acid have, and what anion charge cancels them? Which anion ending gives an -ic acid?

Answer

A. Three H+ cancel the anion's charge, so it is 3−. An -ic acid comes from an -ate ion. (Phosphoric acid, H3PO4, from phosphate, PO43−, fits this pattern.)

  • B has the right charge but the wrong ending: an -ite ion gives an -ous acid.
  • C has the wrong sign. The H+ ions are positive, so the anion must be negative.
  • D confuses the 4 oxygen atoms with the charge, and an -ide ion contains no oxygen and gives a hydro-...-ic acid.

4.6 Putting It Together

Problem 4.43

Classify each compound (ionic, molecular, or acid) and name it: (a) NaClO, household bleach; (b) H2O2; (c) N2O; (d) KMnO4, used in some well-water treatment systems; (e) Cu(OH)2; (f) HNO3; (g) PF5; (h) NH4Cl.

Hint

Follow the blue sheet: metal or NH4 first (ionic; fixed or variable charge?), H first (acid), or nonmetals only (prefixes, or a common name).

Answer
  • (a) Ionic, fixed-charge metal: sodium hypochlorite.
  • (b) Molecular, common name: hydrogen peroxide.
  • (c) Molecular: dinitrogen monoxide (nitrous oxide).
  • (d) Ionic, fixed-charge metal: potassium permanganate.
  • (e) Ionic, variable-charge metal: 2 OH− = 2−, so Cu2+: copper(II) hydroxide (cupric hydroxide).
  • (f) Acid, from nitrate: nitric acid.
  • (g) Molecular: phosphorus pentafluoride.
  • (h) Ionic, ammonium cation: ammonium chloride.

Problem 4.44

Write the formula of each compound: (a) sodium hypochlorite; (b) sulfur tetrafluoride; (c) mercuric chloride; (d) hydrobromic acid; (e) potassium dichromate; (f) aluminum hydroxide, an antacid ingredient; (g) chlorous acid; (h) sodium peroxide.

Hint

Read the clue in each name: numeral or -ous/-ic (variable metal), prefixes (molecular), "acid" (go through the anion). Peroxide is a polyatomic ion on the green sheet.

Answer
  • (a) Na+, ClO−: NaClO.
  • (b) Prefixes, molecular: SF4.
  • (c) Mercuric = mercury(II), Hg2+, with 2 Cl−: HgCl2. (Mercurous chloride, mercury(I), would be Hg2Cl2.)
  • (d) From bromide: HBr(aq).
  • (e) 2 K+, Cr2O72−: K2Cr2O7.
  • (f) Al3+, 3 OH−: Al(OH)3.
  • (g) -ous from chlorite, ClO2−: HClO2.
  • (h) 2 Na+, O22−: Na2O2. Do not reduce it to NaO: O22− is a polyatomic ion, and its subscript never changes.

Problem 4.45

Which compound is named with Greek prefixes?

A. K2O
B. Cl2O
C. Cu2O
D. Na2O2

Hint

Greek prefixes are only for molecular compounds. Which choice has no metal in it?

Answer

B. Chlorine and oxygen are both nonmetals, so Cl2O is molecular: dichlorine monoxide.

  • A is ionic (potassium is a metal): potassium oxide.
  • C is ionic: one O2− over two Cu gives Cu+, copper(I) oxide (cuprous oxide). It uses a Roman numeral, not a prefix.
  • D is ionic: sodium peroxide.

Problem 4.46

Each of these contains sulfur and oxygen. Name each one and say whether it is a molecule, a polyatomic ion, an ionic compound, or an acid: (a) SO3; (b) SO32−; (c) Na2SO3; (d) H2SO3; (e) NaHSO3, a food preservative.

Hint

Look for a charge (ion), a metal first (ionic compound), H first (acid), or a neutral formula of nonmetals only (molecule). Then name it by that type's rules.

Answer
  • (a) Neutral, nonmetals only: a molecule, sulfur trioxide.
  • (b) Has a charge: a polyatomic ion, sulfite.
  • (c) Metal first: an ionic compound, sodium sulfite.
  • (d) Starts with H: an acid, sulfurous acid (sulfite → -ous).
  • (e) Metal first: an ionic compound of Na+ and HSO3−, sodium hydrogen sulfite, or sodium bisulfite.

Problem 4.47

Which formula and name are correctly matched?

A. FeO, iron oxide
B. NO, nitrogen monoxide
C. Na2S, disodium sulfide
D. HClO4, hydroperchloric acid

Hint

Check each against its type's rules: iron needs its charge in the name, ionic names never use prefixes, and oxyacids never get hydro-.

Answer

B. NO is molecular (two nonmetals): one N, no prefix, and one O, mono-. Its common name is nitric oxide.

  • A needs the iron charge: one O2− means Fe2+, so iron(II) oxide (ferrous oxide).
  • C is ionic, so no prefixes: sodium sulfide.
  • D is an oxyacid, so no hydro-: perchloric acid.

Problem 4.48

For each name, state the clue that tells you the type of compound, and write the formula: (a) tin(II) chloride; (b) sulfur dioxide; (c) hydrofluoric acid; (d) ammonium nitrate; (e) chromous chloride.

Hint

The clue is in each name: Roman numeral or -ous ending, Greek prefix, "acid" with hydro-, or ammonium. Then build the formula by that type's rules.

Answer
  • (a) Roman numeral: ionic, variable-charge metal. Sn2+ and 2 Cl−: SnCl2.
  • (b) Greek prefix: molecular. SO2.
  • (c) The word "acid" with hydro-: a binary acid from fluoride. HF(aq).
  • (d) "Ammonium" first: ionic. NH4NO3.
  • (e) An -ous ending on a metal: ionic, the lower charge of chromium, Cr2+. CrCl2, chromium(II) chloride.

Problem 4.49

Nitrate from fertilizer and manure is a drinking-water concern in parts of the Valley, partly because the body can convert nitrate to nitrite. (a) Write the formulas of the nitrate and nitrite ions. (b) Name KNO3, a fertilizer, and NaNO2, used to cure meats. (c) Name the acids HNO3 and HNO2. (d) Write the formula of calcium nitrite.

Hint

(a) Nitrate is on the green sheet; -ite has one less O, same charge. (b) Both metals have fixed charges. (c) -ate becomes -ic, -ite becomes -ous. (d) Balance Ca2+.

Answer

(a) Nitrate, NO3−; nitrite, NO2− (one less O, same charge).

(b) KNO3: potassium nitrate. NaNO2: sodium nitrite.

(c) HNO3: nitric acid (-ate → -ic). HNO2: nitrous acid (-ite → -ous).

(d) Ca2+ needs two NO2−: Ca(NO2)2.

Problem 4.50

A student names Co2(CO3)3 "cobalt(II) carbonate." What is the correct name?

A. cobalt(III) carbonate
B. dicobalt tricarbonate
C. cobaltous carbonate
D. cobalt(III) carbide

Hint

Find cobalt's charge from carbonate: three CO32− carry how much charge, shared by how many cobalt ions? Also check that the anion name matches CO32−.

Answer

A. Three carbonate ions give 3(2−) = 6−, shared by two cobalt ions, so each is Co3+: cobalt(III) carbonate (cobaltic carbonate). The student used the subscript 2 as the charge.

  • B uses prefixes, which are not used for ionic compounds.
  • C means cobalt(II), the same mistake as the student's.
  • D changes the anion. CO32− is carbonate, a polyatomic ion that keeps its name.

Mixed Review

Problem 4.51

An element in period 4 has the valence configuration 4s2. (a) Identify it, and give the charge of its ion. (b) Write the formulas and names of the compounds it forms with bromide and with hydrogen phosphate. (Calcium hydrogen phosphate is a polishing agent in some toothpastes.)

Hint

(a) Period 4 with two valence electrons in 4s: which group and element? It loses its valence electrons. (b) Balance that ion with Br− and with HPO42−.

Answer

(a) Period 4, two valence electrons in 4s: group 2, calcium. It loses both valence electrons to form Ca2+.

(b) With Br−: CaBr2, calcium bromide. With HPO42−: the charges are equal and opposite, so CaHPO4, calcium hydrogen phosphate.

Problem 4.52

Iron (Z = 26) forms the ions in ferrous sulfate, an iron supplement, and in ferric chloride. (a) How many protons and electrons are in Fe2+ and in Fe3+? (b) Write the formulas of iron(II) oxide and iron(III) oxide. (c) Which iron ion is in ferrous sulfate?

Hint

(a) Protons = Z; a 2+ or 3+ ion has lost 2 or 3 electrons. (b) Swap-drop each iron ion with O2−. (c) -ous is the lower charge.

Answer

(a) Both have 26 protons, because the proton count identifies iron. Fe2+ has 26 − 2 = 24 electrons; Fe3+ has 26 − 3 = 23 electrons.

(b) Fe2+ and O2−: FeO. Fe3+ and O2−: Fe2O3.

(c) Fe2+: ferrous is the lower charge (FeSO4, iron(II) sulfate).

Problem 4.53

An ion has 16 protons and 18 electrons. Which choice identifies the ion and correctly gives the formula of its compound with aluminum?

A. S2−; Al2S3
B. S2−; Al3S2
C. Cl−; AlCl3
D. Ar; no compound forms

Hint

Protons identify the element; extra electrons give the negative charge. Then pair that ion with Al3+ using swap-drop.

Answer

A. 16 protons means sulfur, and 2 more electrons than protons gives a 2− charge: S2−, sulfide. With Al3+, swap-drop gives Al2S3 (aluminum sulfide). Check: 2(3+) + 3(2−) = 0.

  • B identifies the ion correctly but does not swap the charges: 3(3+) + 2(2−) = 5+.
  • C is wrong because chlorine has 17 protons, not 16.
  • D is wrong because argon has 18 protons. A particle with 16 protons is sulfur, whatever its electron count; it only has as many electrons as argon.

Problem 4.54

Zinc oxide (Z = 30 for zinc) is the white mineral in many sunscreens and diaper creams. (a) How many protons and electrons are in Zn2+ and in O2−? (b) Write the formula of zinc oxide and show that it is neutral. (c) Why does the name "zinc oxide" not need a Roman numeral, while "copper(II) oxide" does?

Hint

(a) Protons = Z; subtract electrons lost or add electrons gained. (b) Check that the charges cancel. (c) Is zinc on the fixed-charge list? Is copper?

Answer

(a) Zn2+: 30 protons, 28 electrons. O2− (Z = 8): 8 protons, 10 electrons.

(b) ZnO. (2+) + (2−) = 0.

(c) Zinc forms only Zn2+ (it is on the fixed-charge list), so "zinc oxide" can mean only ZnO. Copper forms both Cu+ and Cu2+, so the name must say which: CuO is copper(II) oxide and Cu2O is copper(I) oxide.

Problem 4.55

Element Y is in period 2 and has 6 valence electrons. Element M is in period 3, group 13. (a) Identify Y and M and predict the charge of each one's ion. (b) Write the formula and name of the ionic compound they form, and count its atoms. (c) Y also forms a molecular compound with carbon that has two Y atoms per carbon. Name it.

Hint

(a) Period 2 with 6 valence electrons, and period 3 group 13: which elements? Each gains or loses electrons to reach an octet. (b) Swap-drop. (c) Two nonmetals: use prefixes.

Answer

(a) Y is oxygen: with 6 valence electrons it gains 2 to reach an octet, forming O2−. M is aluminum: with 3 valence electrons it loses them all, forming Al3+.

(b) Swap-drop gives Al2O3, aluminum oxide: 2 Al + 3 O = 5 atoms. Check: 2(3+) + 3(2−) = 0.

(c) CO2, carbon dioxide. Carbon and oxygen are both nonmetals, so the name uses prefixes, not charges.

Problem 4.56

In which compound do both ions have the same electron configuration as neon?

A. NaF
B. KCl
C. MgS
D. CaO

Hint

Count each ion's electrons: subtract for a cation, add for an anion. Neon has 10. Which choice has both ions at that count?

Answer

A. Na+ (11 − 1 = 10 electrons) and F− (9 + 1 = 10 electrons) are both isoelectronic with neon, 1s2 2s2 2p6. The compound is sodium fluoride.

  • B: K+ and Cl− both have 18 electrons, like argon (potassium chloride).
  • C: Mg2+ has 10 electrons (neon), but S2− has 18 (argon) (magnesium sulfide).
  • D: O2− has 10 electrons (neon), but Ca2+ has 18 (argon) (calcium oxide).

More Problems: Memory Aids and Traps (Sections 4.2–4.5)

These problems practice the memory aids and traps added to Sections 4.2–4.5: the T43 pattern, parentheses, Roman numerals read as charges, reduced formulas, bases, and hydrates.

Problem 4.57

Without the green sheet, use the T43 pattern to give the formula and charge of (a) the arsenate ion and (b) the tellurate ion. Then write the formulas of (c) potassium arsenate and (d) aluminum tellurate.

Hint

Find As and Te in the 4 of T43: four O each, with the charge of the ion right above (phosphate, sulfate). Then balance with K+ and with Al3+.

Answer

(a) As is in the "4" (group 15): 4 O atoms and the group 15 charge, AsO43− (it looks like phosphate, right above it).

(b) Te is in the "4" (group 16): TeO42− (like sulfate).

(c) K+ and AsO43−: K3AsO4. One arsenate, so no parentheses.

(d) Al3+ and TeO42−: Al2(TeO4)3. Check: 2(3+) + 3(2−) = 0.

Problem 4.58

What is the formula of cobalt(III) oxide?

A. CoO3
B. Co3O
C. Co2O3
D. CoO

Hint

The Roman numeral is cobalt's charge, not a subscript. Write the cobalt ion and O2− with their charges, then swap-drop.

Answer

C. The Roman numeral is the charge: Co3+. Oxide is O2−. Swap-drop gives Co2O3. Check: 2(3+) + 3(2−) = 0.

  • A treats the numeral III as a subscript on oxygen; CoO3 would need Co6+.
  • B puts the numeral on cobalt as a subscript; three Co3+ and one O2− give 7+, not 0.
  • D is cobalt(II) oxide, Co2+ with O2−.

Problem 4.59

Which formula is written correctly?

A. K2(CO3), potassium carbonate
B. Mg(NO3)2, magnesium nitrate
C. Ba(Cl)2, barium chloride
D. Sn2(SO4)4, tin(IV) sulfate

Hint

Parentheses go only around a polyatomic ion that appears two or more times, and subscripts must be reduced. Test each choice against both rules.

Answer

B. Mg2+ needs two NO3− ions, so the nitrate is in parentheses with a 2 outside.

  • A has the right atoms, but parentheses are used only when the polyatomic ion's subscript is 2 or more. Write K2CO3.
  • C puts parentheses around a single atom. Parentheses are only for polyatomic ions: BaCl2.
  • D was not reduced. The outside subscripts 2 and 4 share a factor of 2: Sn(SO4)2.

Problem 4.60

A student names Sn(SO4)2 by moving each subscript back up as a charge ("reverse swap-drop"). What is the correct charge on tin, and the correct name?

A. Sn2+; tin(II) sulfate
B. Sn4+; tin(IV) sulfate
C. Sn2+; tin disulfate
D. Sn4+; tin(II) sulfate

Hint

Start from the ion you know: sulfate is always 2−. Two sulfates carry how much charge, all on one tin ion? The numeral must equal that charge.

Answer

B. Start from the ion you know: sulfate is always SO42−. Two sulfates carry 2(2−) = 4−, so the single tin ion is Sn4+, tin(IV) sulfate. The formula was reduced from Sn2(SO4)4.

  • A is what un-swapping gives. It would also make sulfate 1−, which it never is.
  • C uses a prefix; ionic compounds never use di-, and the charge is wrong.
  • D has the right charge but the wrong numeral; the numeral must match the charge, IV.

Problem 4.61

Classify each as an acid or a base, then name it: (a) RbOH; (b) Mg(OH)2; (c) HBrO; (d) HBrO3. (e) Write the formula of cesium hydroxide.

Hint

Front or back? H first means an acid (name it from its anion); OH at the end means a base (metal name + hydroxide). For (e), find cesium's group.

Answer

(a) OH in back: base, rubidium hydroxide. (b) Base, magnesium hydroxide (milk of magnesia); no prefix, even though there are two hydroxides. (c) H in front: acid. The anion BrO− is hypobromite (two fewer O than bromate), so the acid is hypobromous acid. (d) Acid from bromate, BrO3−: bromic acid. (e) Cs+ and OH−: CsOH.

Problem 4.62

Some humidity indicator cards contain cobalt(II) chloride, which is pink as the hexahydrate and blue when it is anhydrous. (a) Write the formula of cobalt(II) chloride hexahydrate. (b) What does "anhydrous" mean, and what is the formula of the anhydrous compound? (c) A classmate finds the molar mass of the hexahydrate by multiplying the mass of CoCl2 by the mass of 6H2O. What is wrong?

Hint

(a) Write the salt, a raised dot, then the waters. (b) "An-" means without and "hydrous" means water. (c) In a hydrate, does the dot mean add or multiply?

Answer

(a) Co2+ and two Cl−, plus six waters: CoCl2·6H2O.

(b) "Without water": the waters have been driven off, leaving CoCl2.

(c) The dot is not a multiplication sign. The molar mass is the mass of CoCl2 plus the mass of six water molecules (Section 5.2).

Problem 4.63

A bottle in the stockroom is labeled MnSO4. What is its correct name?

A. magnesium sulfate
B. manganese sulfate
C. manganese(II) sulfate
D. magnesium(II) sulfate

Hint

Mn or Mg? Check the symbol on the periodic table. Then ask whether that metal has a fixed charge or needs a Roman numeral.

Answer

C. Mn is manganese, a transition metal that forms more than one ion, so it needs a Roman numeral. Sulfate is 2−, so the manganese is 2+: manganese(II) sulfate.

  • A and D name the wrong metal. Magnesium is Mg (MgSO4, the electrolyte Mateo received by IV (intravenously)), and as a group 2 metal it never takes a numeral, so D is wrong twice.
  • B is the right metal, but leaving off the numeral does not say which manganese ion is present.

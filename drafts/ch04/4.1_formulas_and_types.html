Learning Objectives

  • Read a chemical formula and count the atoms of each element, including atoms inside parentheses.
  • Recognize the seven elements that exist as diatomic molecules.
  • Classify a compound as ionic, molecular, or an acid from its formula.

A chemical formula is a short code that tells you which elements are in a substance and how many atoms of each. Water is H2O, table salt is NaCl, and the calcium mineral in your bones and teeth is built from Ca2+, PO43−, and OH− ions. Before you can name anything, you need to read formulas fluently.

Reading a Formula

  • Each element symbol stands for one atom of that element.
  • A subscript (small number written low, after a symbol) tells how many atoms of the element just before it. No subscript means 1.
  • A subscript after parentheses multiplies everything inside them. In Ca(NO3)2, there are 2 nitrate groups, so 2 N atoms and 2 × 3 = 6 O atoms.
  • A number in front of a formula, called a coefficient, counts whole formula units; you will use coefficients in Chapter 7. 3 H2O means three water molecules: 6 H and 3 O atoms.

Example \(\PageIndex{1}\): Counting atoms

Problem. How many atoms of each element are in (a) Al2(SO4)3 and (b) (NH4)3PO4?

Solution. (a) Al: 2. S: 1 × 3 = 3. O: 4 × 3 = 12. Total 17 atoms. (b) N: 1 × 3 = 3. H: 4 × 3 = 12. P: 1. O: 4. Total 20 atoms.

Check. Multiply only what is inside the parentheses; the P and O in (b) are outside, so they are not multiplied by 3.

Elements that come in pairs

Most elements are written as single symbols (Fe, Na, C), but seven elements exist as diatomic molecules, two atoms bonded together, when they are uncombined: H2, N2, O2, F2, Cl2, Br2, and I2. On the periodic table, they make a "7" shape starting at element 7 (N, O, F, then down Cl, Br, I), plus hydrogen. The oxygen you breathe is O2; the chlorine used to disinfect water is Cl2.

Three Kinds of Compounds

Naming rules depend on what kind of compound you have, so the first question is always "what type is it?"

  • Ionic compounds are made of ions: a cation (usually a metal, or the ammonium ion NH4+) and an anion (a nonmetal or a polyatomic anion). Their formulas start with a metal or NH4. The opposite charges attract, forming ionic bonds. An ionic formula gives the simplest ratio of ions, called a formula unit, because there are no separate molecules. Examples: NaCl, CaCO3, NH4NO3.
  • Molecular compounds (also called covalent compounds) are made only of nonmetals, whose atoms share electrons in covalent bonds. They exist as separate molecules. Examples: H2O, CO2, N2O.
  • Acids are molecular compounds whose formulas start with H and which release H+ ions when dissolved in water, shown by (aq) for "aqueous." Examples: HCl(aq), HNO3, H2SO4.

Example \(\PageIndex{2}\): Classify each compound

Problem. Classify each as ionic, molecular, or an acid: (a) K2S; (b) SO2; (c) HBr(aq); (d) NH4Cl; (e) Fe(OH)3.

Solution. (a) K is a metal: ionic. (b) S and O are both nonmetals: molecular. (c) starts with H and is aqueous: acid. (d) starts with NH4+: ionic, even though every element is a nonmetal. (e) Fe is a metal: ionic (OH− is the hydroxide ion; a formula ending in OH is not an acid).

Check. Look at the first part of the formula: metal or NH4 means ionic, H (aq) means acid, otherwise all nonmetals means molecular.

Professor Tip

Keep the periodic table (yellow sheet) in front of you and find the staircase line. Everything to its left, except hydrogen, is a metal. If the first symbol in a formula is left of the staircase, you are naming an ionic compound. Ammonium (NH4+) is the one cation in this course that has no metal in it, so learn to spot it.

Biology Connection: Anatomy and Physiology

Your body is full of both kinds of compounds. Bone and tooth enamel are mostly hydroxyapatite, an ionic solid built from calcium ions (Ca2+), phosphate ions (PO43−), and hydroxide ions (OH−), with the formula Ca10(PO4)6(OH)2. Blood plasma carries dissolved ions such as Na+, Cl−, and bicarbonate (HCO3−). Molecular compounds are everywhere too: water, the O2 and CO2 you exchange in your lungs, and glucose (C6H12O6). Your stomach even makes an acid, hydrochloric acid, HCl(aq), to start digesting proteins.

Common Mistakes

  • Forgetting to multiply inside parentheses. Mg(OH)2 has 2 O and 2 H atoms, not 1 of each.
  • Calling ammonium compounds molecular. NH4Cl contains ions, so it is ionic.
  • Calling hydroxides acids. NaOH ends in OH (hydroxide); acids start with H.
  • Writing O instead of O2 for oxygen gas. The seven diatomic elements are written with a subscript 2 when uncombined.

Summary

  • Subscripts count atoms of the element before them; a subscript after parentheses multiplies everything inside.
  • H2, N2, O2, F2, Cl2, Br2, and I2 are diatomic.
  • Ionic: starts with a metal or NH4+. Molecular: nonmetals only. Acid: starts with H and dissolves in water to release H+.

Exercises

Exercise \(\PageIndex{1}\)

How many oxygen atoms are in Fe2(CO3)3?

Answer

3 × 3 = 9 oxygen atoms.

Exercise \(\PageIndex{2}\)

Classify each as ionic, molecular, or an acid: CCl4, MgO, HNO3(aq), (NH4)2SO4.

Answer

CCl4: molecular (two nonmetals). MgO: ionic (metal). HNO3(aq): acid. (NH4)2SO4: ionic (ammonium cation).

Exercise \(\PageIndex{3}\)

Which of these elements are diatomic when uncombined: neon, nitrogen, sodium, bromine?

Answer

Nitrogen (N2) and bromine (Br2). Neon is a single-atom noble gas, and sodium is a metal.

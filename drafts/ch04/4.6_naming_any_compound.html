Learning Objectives

  • Decide whether a formula or name belongs to an ionic compound, a molecular compound, or an acid.
  • Apply the right set of naming rules to any compound in this course.
  • Tell apart a molecule, a polyatomic ion, an ionic compound, and an acid that contain the same group of atoms.

You now have three sets of rules. The hard part on an exam is not any single rule; it is choosing the right one quickly. This section puts everything in one flowchart and then works through the cases that cause the most confusion.

The Decision Flowchart

When you start from a name, the clues are in the words: a metal name or "ammonium" first means ionic; Greek prefixes mean molecular; the word "acid" means an acid; a Roman numeral means a variable-charge metal.

Example \(\PageIndex{1}\): A mixed set

Problem. Name each compound: (a) Ca(ClO)2; (b) Cl2O; (c) HClO(aq); (d) CuCl2; (e) NH4HCO3; (f) N2H4.

Solution.

  • (a) Metal first, fixed charge: ionic. Ca2+ and hypochlorite: calcium hypochlorite (used to chlorinate pools).
  • (b) Two nonmetals: molecular. dichlorine monoxide.
  • (c) Starts with H, aqueous, anion hypochlorite: hypochlorous acid.
  • (d) Variable-charge metal: 2 Cl− = 2−, so Cu2+: copper(II) chloride.
  • (e) Starts with ammonium: ionic. ammonium bicarbonate (ammonium hydrogen carbonate).
  • (f) Two nonmetals: molecular. dinitrogen tetrahydride (its common name is hydrazine).

Check. Prefixes appear only in (b) and (f), the two molecular compounds; the Roman numeral appears only on copper, the only variable-charge metal.

Same Atoms, Different Species

The same group of atoms can show up as a molecule, an ion, part of an ionic compound, or an acid. The charge and the rest of the formula tell you which, and each gets a different kind of name:

  • ClO2: a neutral molecule of two nonmetals, chlorine dioxide (a water disinfectant).
  • ClO2−: a polyatomic ion, chlorite.
  • NaClO2: an ionic compound, sodium chlorite.
  • HClO2: an acid, chlorous acid.

Likewise, H2SO4 is sulfuric acid, HSO4− is the hydrogen sulfate (bisulfate) ion, and NaHSO4 is sodium hydrogen sulfate (sodium bisulfate), an ingredient in some toilet-bowl cleaners. Hydroxides such as NaOH contain H but are ionic because they start with a metal; they are named like any other ionic compound (sodium hydroxide).

Professor Tip

Say the type out loud before you name: "metal first, ionic"; "two nonmetals, prefixes"; "H in water, acid." It takes one second and prevents the most common errors, such as "dicopper oxide" or "sulfur oxide." For practice, cover the answers in this chapter's examples and name the compounds in a random order, because exams will not group them by type.

Biology Connection: Microbiology

Labs and clinics disinfect with several compounds whose names sound alike. Sodium hypochlorite, NaClO, is household bleach, an ionic compound; its active form in water is hypochlorous acid, HClO, which kills bacteria and viruses by damaging their proteins. Hydrogen peroxide, H2O2, is a molecular compound used to disinfect surfaces and, at low concentration, minor wounds. Chlorine dioxide, ClO2, treats drinking water. These are different compounds with different strengths, contact times, and hazards, and mixing bleach with acids or with ammonia releases toxic gases. Reading the full name on the label is the first safety step in any microbiology lab.

Common Mistakes

  • Naming before classifying. Always decide ionic, molecular, or acid first.
  • Treating ammonium compounds as molecular. (NH4)2S is ammonium sulfide, not "diammonium sulfide."
  • Treating hydroxides as acids. KOH is potassium hydroxide.
  • Confusing a molecule with an ion. ClO2 (chlorine dioxide) is not ClO2− (chlorite).

Summary

  • Classify first: H in water = acid; metal or NH4+ first = ionic; nonmetals only = molecular.
  • Ionic: cation + anion, Roman numerals only for variable-charge metals, no prefixes. Molecular: Greek prefixes. Acids: -ide → hydro-...-ic, -ate → -ic, -ite → -ous.
  • The same atoms can form a molecule, an ion, an ionic compound, or an acid, each with its own name.

Exercises

Exercise \(\PageIndex{1}\)

Name (a) SO2; (b) K2SO3; (c) H2SO3; (d) Fe2(SO4)3.

Answer

(a) sulfur dioxide (molecular). (b) potassium sulfite (ionic). (c) sulfurous acid (sulfite → -ous). (d) iron(III) sulfate: 3 sulfates = 6− over 2 iron ions.

Exercise \(\PageIndex{2}\)

Write formulas for (a) nitrogen trifluoride; (b) magnesium nitride; (c) nitric acid; (d) ammonium nitrite.

Answer

(a) NF3. (b) Mg3N2. (c) HNO3. (d) NH4NO2.

Exercise \(\PageIndex{3}\)

Explain why CaCl2 is not named "calcium dichloride" but CCl4 is named "carbon tetrachloride."

Answer

CaCl2 is ionic (calcium is a metal); the fixed charges already fix the ratio, so no prefixes are used. CCl4 is molecular (two nonmetals); there are no charges to fix the ratio, so prefixes give the atom counts.

Learning Objectives

  • Use the stoichiometry road map to convert grams of one substance into grams of another.
  • Combine molar masses and mole ratios in one setup.

In the lab and the pharmacy, nobody measures moles directly; they weigh grams. A mass-to-mass problem asks, "If I start with this many grams of A, how many grams of B can I make (or do I need)?" The answer always goes through moles, because only moles have a ratio in the balanced equation. As the lectures put it: our goal is the mole.

The Road Map

  1. Balance the equation.
  2. grams of A → moles of A (divide by the molar mass of A).
  3. moles of A → moles of B (mole ratio from the coefficients).
  4. moles of B → grams of B (multiply by the molar mass of B).

Example \(\PageIndex{1}\): CO2 from glucose

Problem. How many grams of CO2 are produced when your cells completely oxidize 25.0 g of glucose? (C6H12O6 180.156 g/mol; CO2 44.009 g/mol)

Solution. C6H12O6 + 6 O2 → 6 CO2 + 6 H2O.

\( 25.0\ \text{g C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 \times \dfrac{1\ \text{mol C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6}{180.156\ \text{g}} \times \dfrac{6\ \text{mol CO}_2}{1\ \text{mol C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6} \times \dfrac{44.009\ \text{g CO}_2}{1\ \text{mol CO}_2} = 36.6\ \text{g CO}_2 \)

Check. The CO2 outweighs the glucose because oxygen from the air is added. By conservation of mass, 25.0 g glucose + 26.6 g O2 = 51.6 g = 36.6 g CO2 + 15.0 g H2O.

Example \(\PageIndex{2}\): Ammonia for fertilizer

Problem. How many grams of NH3 can be made from 100. g of N2 and plenty of H2? (N2 28.014 g/mol; NH3 17.031 g/mol)

Solution. N2 + 3 H2 → 2 NH3.

\( 100.\ \text{g N}_2 \times \dfrac{1\ \text{mol N}_2}{28.014\ \text{g}} \times \dfrac{2\ \text{mol NH}_3}{1\ \text{mol N}_2} \times \dfrac{17.031\ \text{g NH}_3}{1\ \text{mol NH}_3} = 122\ \text{g NH}_3 \)

Check. All of the nitrogen ends up in ammonia, plus the added hydrogen, so a little more than 100 g of product makes sense.

Professor Tip

Two pitfalls from the lectures: never put a coefficient into a molar mass (the molar mass of K is 39.10 g/mol even when the equation says 2 K), and never "add mol" to a given number that was in grams. Set up the whole chain of factors in one line, check that every unit cancels except the one you want, and press enter once on the TI-36X Pro.

Biology Connection: Microbiology

Yeast running out of oxygen switch to fermentation: C6H12O6 → 2 C2H5OH + 2 CO2. Bakers rely on the CO2 to raise bread; brewers and winemakers in California rely on the ethanol. The same stoichiometry applies: 50.0 g of glucose can produce at most 25.6 g of ethanol and 24.4 g of CO2. Many bacteria ferment too, and microbiology labs identify species partly by which acids and gases they produce from a sugar, for example by watching for gas bubbles trapped in a small inverted tube in the growth broth.

Common Mistakes

  • Skipping moles: grams of A × mole ratio is meaningless.
  • Using the wrong molar mass at each end: A's molar mass first, B's last.
  • Multiplying a molar mass by a coefficient.

Summary

  • grams A → mol A → mol B → grams B, using molar mass, mole ratio, molar mass.
  • Always balance first; always pass through moles.

Exercises

Exercise \(\PageIndex{1}\)

How many grams of O2 are needed to burn 10.0 g of methane? CH4 + 2 O2 → CO2 + 2 H2O (CH4 16.043, O2 31.998 g/mol)

Answer

10.0/16.043 = 0.6233 mol CH4; × 2 = 1.247 mol O2; × 31.998 = 39.9 g O2.

Exercise \(\PageIndex{2}\)

How many grams of H2O form when 36.6 g of CO2 form from glucose (Example 1)?

Answer

The ratio is 6 CO2 : 6 H2O, so equal moles: 36.6/44.009 = 0.8316 mol; × 18.015 = 15.0 g H2O.

Exercise \(\PageIndex{3}\)

Iron is made from iron ore: Fe2O3 + 3 CO → 2 Fe + 3 CO2. How many grams of Fe come from 500. g of Fe2O3 (159.70 g/mol)?

Answer

500./159.70 = 3.131 mol; × 2 = 6.262 mol Fe; × 55.85 = 350. g Fe.

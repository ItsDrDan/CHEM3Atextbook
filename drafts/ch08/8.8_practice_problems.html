8.1 Mole Ratios from Balanced Equations

Problem 8.1

Natural gas contains a little ethane, C2H6, which burns by the reaction below.

2 C2H6(g) + 7 O2(g) → 4 CO2(g) + 6 H2O(g)

Which conversion factor turns moles of ethane into moles of O2 needed?

A. \( \dfrac{7\ \text{mol O}_2}{2\ \text{mol C}_2\text{H}_6} \)
B. \( \dfrac{2\ \text{mol C}_2\text{H}_6}{7\ \text{mol O}_2} \)
C. \( \dfrac{1\ \text{mol O}_2}{1\ \text{mol C}_2\text{H}_6} \)
D. \( \dfrac{7\ \text{g O}_2}{2\ \text{g C}_2\text{H}_6} \)

Answer

A. The coefficients say 2 mol C2H6 react with 7 mol O2. To cancel mol C2H6 and leave mol O2, put O2 (the unit you want) on top.

  • B is the same ratio flipped. It is a true ratio, but multiplying moles of ethane by it leaves mol C2H62/mol O2, not mol O2.
  • C ignores the coefficients. A 1 : 1 ratio is only correct when both coefficients are equal.
  • D treats the coefficients as grams. Coefficients count molecules and moles, not masses; an O2 molecule and a C2H6 molecule have different masses.

Problem 8.2

Some bottled-water plants disinfect water with ozone, made from oxygen by an electric discharge:

3 O2(g) → 2 O3(g)

How many moles of ozone can be made from 0.0450 mol of O2?

Answer

\( 0.0450\ \text{mol O}_2 \times \dfrac{2\ \text{mol O}_3}{3\ \text{mol O}_2} = 0.0300\ \text{mol O}_3 \)

The answer is 0.0300 mol O3, three significant figures like 0.0450 (the mole ratio is exact). Fewer moles of O3 form than moles of O2 used, because each O3 holds more oxygen atoms; the 0.135 mol of O atoms is the same on both sides.

Problem 8.3

The leaves of a Valley almond tree make glucose by photosynthesis:

6 CO2(g) + 6 H2O(l) → C6H12O6(aq) + 6 O2(g)

A leaf sample makes 0.00250 mol of glucose. (a) How many moles of CO2 did it absorb? (b) How many moles of O2 did it release?

Answer

(a) \( 0.00250\ \text{mol C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 \times \dfrac{6\ \text{mol CO}_2}{1\ \text{mol C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6} = 0.0150\ \text{mol CO}_2 \)

(b) \( 0.00250\ \text{mol C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 \times \dfrac{6\ \text{mol O}_2}{1\ \text{mol C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6} = 0.0150\ \text{mol O}_2 \)

(a) 0.0150 mol CO2 absorbed; (b) 0.0150 mol O2 released. Photosynthesis is cellular respiration run backward, so the plant takes in one CO2 for every O2 it gives off.

Problem 8.4

Yeast ferment glucose into ethanol and carbon dioxide:

C6H12O6(aq) → 2 C2H5OH(aq) + 2 CO2(g)

How many moles of glucose must be fermented to make 3.40 mol of ethanol?

A. 1.70 mol
B. 6.80 mol
C. 3.40 mol
D. 0.567 mol

Answer

A.

\( 3.40\ \text{mol C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} \times \dfrac{1\ \text{mol C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6}{2\ \text{mol C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}} = 1.70\ \text{mol C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 \)

Each glucose makes two ethanol molecules, so you need half as many moles of glucose as the ethanol you want.

  • B uses the flipped ratio (2 mol glucose / 1 mol ethanol), which would need more glucose than ethanol made.
  • C skips the mole ratio, as if 1 glucose made 1 ethanol.
  • D divides by the subscript 6 in C6H12O6. Mole ratios between substances come from the coefficients, not from the subscripts inside a formula.

Problem 8.5

Aluminum cookware is protected by a thin layer of aluminum oxide that forms in air:

4 Al(s) + 3 O2(g) → 2 Al2O3(s)

For 0.600 mol of Al, find (a) the moles of O2 that react and (b) the moles of Al2O3 that form.

Answer

(a) \( 0.600\ \text{mol Al} \times \dfrac{3\ \text{mol O}_2}{4\ \text{mol Al}} = 0.450\ \text{mol O}_2 \)

(b) \( 0.600\ \text{mol Al} \times \dfrac{2\ \text{mol Al}_2\text{O}_3}{4\ \text{mol Al}} = 0.300\ \text{mol Al}_2\text{O}_3 \)

(a) 0.450 mol O2; (b) 0.300 mol Al2O3. Check with the atoms: 0.300 mol Al2O3 holds 0.600 mol Al, all of the aluminum you started with.

Problem 8.6

Gasoline is mostly octane. In a farm truck's engine, octane burns completely by

2 C8H18(l) + 25 O2(g) → 16 CO2(g) + 18 H2O(g)

For 0.880 mol of octane burned, how many moles of (a) O2 are used, (b) CO2 form, and (c) H2O form?

Answer

Every ratio has 2 mol C8H18 on the bottom:

  • (a) 0.880 mol × (25 mol O2 / 2 mol C8H18) = 11.0 mol O2
  • (b) 0.880 mol × (16 mol CO2 / 2 mol C8H18) = 7.04 mol CO2
  • (c) 0.880 mol × (18 mol H2O / 2 mol C8H18) = 7.92 mol H2O

Check (b) with the atoms: each octane has 8 C atoms, and 0.880 × 8 = 7.04 mol of C, which all ends up in CO2.

Problem 8.7

Sulfur dioxide from burning fuels is turned into sulfur trioxide in the air:

2 SO2(g) + O2(g) → 2 SO3(g)

Which statement is correct?

A. 2 g of SO2 react with 1 g of O2 to make 2 g of SO3.
B. 2 mol of SO2 react with 1 mol of O2 to make 2 mol of SO3.
C. Moles are conserved, so 3 mol of reactants make 3 mol of products.
D. 1 mol of SO2 makes 2 mol of SO3.

Answer

B. Coefficients give mole ratios: 2 mol SO2 : 1 mol O2 : 2 mol SO3.

  • A reads the coefficients as grams. Also, 2 g + 1 g = 3 g of reactants could not make only 2 g of product; mass is conserved.
  • C: atoms and mass are conserved, but moles of molecules are not. Here 3 mol of reactant molecules make only 2 mol of product molecules.
  • D misreads the ratio. SO2 and SO3 have the same coefficient, so 1 mol of SO2 makes 1 mol of SO3.

Problem 8.8

Iron metal reacts with chlorine gas to make iron(III) chloride. A student writes the unbalanced equation below and says 0.840 mol of Fe needs 0.840 mol of Cl2.

Fe(s) + Cl2(g) → FeCl3(s)    (unbalanced)

(a) Balance the equation. (b) How many moles of Cl2 does 0.840 mol of Fe really need? (c) How many moles of FeCl3 form?

Answer

(a) FeCl3 has 3 Cl atoms and Cl2 has 2, so the smallest common count of Cl is 6: put 2 in front of FeCl3 and 3 in front of Cl2, then 2 in front of Fe.

2 Fe(s) + 3 Cl2(g) → 2 FeCl3(s)

(b) \( 0.840\ \text{mol Fe} \times \dfrac{3\ \text{mol Cl}_2}{2\ \text{mol Fe}} = 1.26\ \text{mol Cl}_2 \)

(c) Fe and FeCl3 have the same coefficient: 0.840 mol FeCl3.

The student's 0.840 mol of Cl2 came from the unbalanced equation, which has a 1 : 1 ratio that is not real. Balance first, every time; the correct amount, 1.26 mol Cl2, is 50% more.

8.2 Mass-to-Mass Stoichiometry

Problem 8.9

A 473 mL bottle of 3% hydrogen peroxide, used to clean minor wounds, contains about 14.2 g of H2O2. The bubbles you see on a cut are oxygen, made as the enzyme catalase speeds up the decomposition below. What mass of O2 could the whole bottle release? (H2O2 34.014 g/mol; O2 31.998 g/mol)

2 H2O2(aq) → 2 H2O(l) + O2(g)

Answer

\( 14.2\ \text{g H}_2\text{O}_2 \times \dfrac{1\ \text{mol H}_2\text{O}_2}{34.014\ \text{g H}_2\text{O}_2} \times \dfrac{1\ \text{mol O}_2}{2\ \text{mol H}_2\text{O}_2} \times \dfrac{31.998\ \text{g O}_2}{1\ \text{mol O}_2} = 6.68\ \text{g O}_2 \)

The bottle could release at most 6.68 g of O2. Check: the rest of the mass, 14.2 − 6.68 = 7.52 g, becomes water, which matches 0.4175 mol H2O × 18.015 g/mol = 7.52 g.

Problem 8.10

Alcohol-based hand sanitizer is flammable because ethanol burns:

C2H5OH(l) + 3 O2(g) → 2 CO2(g) + 3 H2O(g)

What mass of O2 is needed to burn 5.00 g of ethanol? (C2H5OH 46.069 g/mol; O2 31.998 g/mol)

A. 10.4 g
B. 3.47 g
C. 1.16 g
D. 31.3 g

Answer

A.

\( 5.00\ \text{g C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} \times \dfrac{1\ \text{mol C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}}{46.069\ \text{g}} \times \dfrac{3\ \text{mol O}_2}{1\ \text{mol C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}} \times \dfrac{31.998\ \text{g O}_2}{1\ \text{mol O}_2} = 10.4\ \text{g O}_2 \)

  • B leaves out the mole ratio (0.1085 mol × 31.998 g/mol), as if 1 mol of ethanol used 1 mol of O2.
  • C uses the flipped ratio, 1 mol ethanol / 3 mol O2.
  • D multiplies the molar mass of O2 by the coefficient (3 × 31.998 = 95.994 g/mol) and uses the 3 : 1 mole ratio, counting the 3 twice. The molar mass of O2 is always 31.998 g/mol.

Problem 8.11

Urea, CO(NH2)2, is one of the most widely used nitrogen fertilizers on Valley crops. It is made from ammonia and carbon dioxide:

2 NH3(g) + CO2(g) → CO(NH2)2(s) + H2O(l)

What mass of urea can be made from 85.0 g of NH3 with excess CO2? (NH3 17.031 g/mol; CO(NH2)2 60.056 g/mol)

Answer

\( 85.0\ \text{g NH}_3 \times \dfrac{1\ \text{mol NH}_3}{17.031\ \text{g NH}_3} \times \dfrac{1\ \text{mol urea}}{2\ \text{mol NH}_3} \times \dfrac{60.056\ \text{g urea}}{1\ \text{mol urea}} = 150.\ \text{g urea} \)

The calculator shows 149.87, which rounds to three significant figures as 150. g. The decimal point matters: written as "150 g," the zero would not be significant and the answer would look like it had only two significant figures.

Problem 8.12

An anesthesia machine recycles the patient's exhaled breath through a canister of CO2 absorbent so that the carbon dioxide does not build up. Treat the absorbent as calcium hydroxide:

Ca(OH)2(s) + CO2(g) → CaCO3(s) + H2O(l)

What mass of CO2 can 1.00 × 103 g of Ca(OH)2 absorb? (Ca(OH)2 74.09 g/mol; CO2 44.009 g/mol)

Answer

\( 1.00 \times 10^{3}\ \text{g Ca(OH)}_2 \times \dfrac{1\ \text{mol Ca(OH)}_2}{74.09\ \text{g}} \times \dfrac{1\ \text{mol CO}_2}{1\ \text{mol Ca(OH)}_2} \times \dfrac{44.009\ \text{g CO}_2}{1\ \text{mol CO}_2} = 594\ \text{g CO}_2 \)

The canister can absorb about 594 g of CO2, roughly what a resting adult exhales in 15 to 16 hours. Real absorbents (soda lime) are mostly Ca(OH)2 with a little water and NaOH, and a color indicator in the granules changes as they are used up. (Context only; follow the equipment and facility procedures.)

Problem 8.13

What mass of O2 do your cells need to completely oxidize 15.0 g of glucose? Check your answer with conservation of mass. (C6H12O6 180.156 g/mol; O2 31.998 g/mol; CO2 44.009 g/mol; H2O 18.015 g/mol)

C6H12O6(aq) + 6 O2(g) → 6 CO2(g) + 6 H2O(l)

Answer

\( 15.0\ \text{g C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 \times \dfrac{1\ \text{mol C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6}{180.156\ \text{g}} \times \dfrac{6\ \text{mol O}_2}{1\ \text{mol C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6} \times \dfrac{31.998\ \text{g O}_2}{1\ \text{mol O}_2} = 16.0\ \text{g O}_2 \)

Your cells need 16.0 g of O2. Check: 0.08326 mol glucose makes 0.4996 mol each of CO2 (22.0 g) and H2O (9.00 g). Reactants: 15.0 + 16.0 = 31.0 g. Products: 22.0 + 9.00 = 31.0 g. Mass is conserved.

Problem 8.14

Railroad crews weld steel rails with the thermite reaction, which makes molten iron:

Fe2O3(s) + 2 Al(s) → Al2O3(s) + 2 Fe(l)

What mass of aluminum reacts completely with 20.0 g of Fe2O3? (Fe2O3 159.70 g/mol; Al 26.98 g/mol)

A. 6.76 g
B. 1.69 g
C. 3.38 g
D. 40.0 g

Answer

A.

\( 20.0\ \text{g Fe}_2\text{O}_3 \times \dfrac{1\ \text{mol Fe}_2\text{O}_3}{159.70\ \text{g}} \times \dfrac{2\ \text{mol Al}}{1\ \text{mol Fe}_2\text{O}_3} \times \dfrac{26.98\ \text{g Al}}{1\ \text{mol Al}} = 6.76\ \text{g Al} \)

  • B uses the flipped ratio, 1 mol Fe2O3 / 2 mol Al.
  • C leaves out the mole ratio (0.1252 mol × 26.98 g/mol).
  • D skips moles entirely and treats the coefficients as grams: 20.0 g × 2 = 40.0 g. Grams of A never go straight to grams of B.

Problem 8.15

A student finds the mass of MgO made when 3.00 g of magnesium burns, 2 Mg(s) + O2(g) → 2 MgO(s), and writes

\( 3.00\ \text{g Mg} \times \dfrac{1\ \text{mol Mg}}{24.31\ \text{g}} \times \dfrac{2\ \text{mol MgO}}{2\ \text{mol Mg}} \times \dfrac{2(40.31)\ \text{g MgO}}{1\ \text{mol MgO}} = 9.95\ \text{g MgO} \)

(a) Find the mistake. (b) What is the correct mass? (c) How does conservation of mass show that 9.95 g is impossible? (Mg 24.31 g/mol; MgO 40.31 g/mol; O2 31.998 g/mol)

Answer

(a) The student multiplied the molar mass by the coefficient. The coefficient 2 is already used in the mole ratio; the molar mass of MgO is 40.31 g/mol no matter what the equation says.

(b) \( 3.00\ \text{g Mg} \times \dfrac{1\ \text{mol Mg}}{24.31\ \text{g}} \times \dfrac{2\ \text{mol MgO}}{2\ \text{mol Mg}} \times \dfrac{40.31\ \text{g MgO}}{1\ \text{mol MgO}} = 4.97\ \text{g MgO} \)

(c) The O2 used is 0.1234 mol Mg × (1/2) × 31.998 g/mol = 1.97 g, so the product can weigh only 3.00 + 1.97 = 4.97 g. To make 9.95 g, the magnesium would have to pick up 6.95 g of oxygen, more than three times what the equation allows.

Problem 8.16

In a classic lab demonstration, heating potassium chlorate with a manganese dioxide catalyst releases oxygen gas:

2 KClO3(s) → 2 KCl(s) + 3 O2(g)

A sample of 4.90 g of KClO3 decomposes completely. Find (a) the mass of O2 released and (b) the mass of KCl left in the test tube. (KClO3 122.55 g/mol; KCl 74.55 g/mol; O2 31.998 g/mol)

Answer

Moles of KClO3: 4.90 g ÷ 122.55 g/mol = 0.03998 mol.

(a) \( 0.03998\ \text{mol KClO}_3 \times \dfrac{3\ \text{mol O}_2}{2\ \text{mol KClO}_3} \times \dfrac{31.998\ \text{g O}_2}{1\ \text{mol O}_2} = 1.92\ \text{g O}_2 \)

(b) \( 0.03998\ \text{mol KClO}_3 \times \dfrac{2\ \text{mol KCl}}{2\ \text{mol KClO}_3} \times \dfrac{74.55\ \text{g KCl}}{1\ \text{mol KCl}} = 2.98\ \text{g KCl} \)

(a) 1.92 g O2; (b) 2.98 g KCl. Check: 1.92 + 2.98 = 4.90 g, the mass you started with. The catalyst is not used up, so it is not part of the calculation.

8.3 Solutions and Gases in Stoichiometry

Problem 8.17

An antacid tablet contains 0.312 g of aluminum hydroxide. What volume of 0.150 M HCl, in milliliters, can it neutralize? (Al(OH)3 78.00 g/mol)

Al(OH)3(s) + 3 HCl(aq) → AlCl3(aq) + 3 H2O(l)

Answer

\( 0.312\ \text{g Al(OH)}_3 \times \dfrac{1\ \text{mol Al(OH)}_3}{78.00\ \text{g}} \times \dfrac{3\ \text{mol HCl}}{1\ \text{mol Al(OH)}_3} \times \dfrac{1\ \text{L}}{0.150\ \text{mol HCl}} = 0.0800\ \text{L} \)

0.0800 L × 1000 mL/L = 80.0 mL of 0.150 M HCl. Dividing moles by molarity (mol/L) gives liters; convert to mL only at the end.

Problem 8.18

In Experiment 8a (Gas Production), a 0.0365 g piece of magnesium ribbon reacts completely with excess hydrochloric acid. What volume of H2 forms, corrected to STP? (Mg 24.31 g/mol)

Mg(s) + 2 HCl(aq) → MgCl2(aq) + H2(g)

A. 33.6 mL
B. 67.3 mL
C. 818 mL
D. 33.6 L

Answer

A.

\( 0.0365\ \text{g Mg} \times \dfrac{1\ \text{mol Mg}}{24.31\ \text{g}} \times \dfrac{1\ \text{mol H}_2}{1\ \text{mol Mg}} \times \dfrac{22.4\ \text{L H}_2}{1\ \text{mol H}_2} = 0.0336\ \text{L} = 33.6\ \text{mL} \)

A few tens of milliliters is the right size for the gas-measuring tube used in the lab.

  • B uses the 2 in front of HCl as the Mg-to-H2 ratio. Mg and H2 each have a coefficient of 1.
  • C multiplies grams by 22.4 L/mol without converting to moles first (0.0365 × 22.4 = 0.818 L). The molar volume applies to moles of gas, not grams of metal.
  • D is the right digits with the wrong unit: the calculation gives 0.0336 L, which is 33.6 mL, not 33.6 L.

Problem 8.19

Stomach acid is roughly 0.1 M HCl. What mass of sodium hydrogen carbonate (baking soda) reacts completely with 30.0 mL of 0.120 M HCl? (NaHCO3 84.01 g/mol)

NaHCO3(s) + HCl(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H2O(l) + CO2(g)

Answer

Convert mL to L first: 30.0 mL = 0.0300 L.

\( 0.0300\ \text{L} \times \dfrac{0.120\ \text{mol HCl}}{1\ \text{L}} \times \dfrac{1\ \text{mol NaHCO}_3}{1\ \text{mol HCl}} \times \dfrac{84.01\ \text{g NaHCO}_3}{1\ \text{mol NaHCO}_3} = 0.302\ \text{g NaHCO}_3 \)

The answer is 0.302 g of baking soda. The CO2 it makes is why baking soda causes burping. (Context only; this is not dosing guidance.)

Problem 8.20

In which case can a student correctly use 22.4 L/mol to convert between volume and moles?

A. 2.00 L of N2 gas at 0 °C and 1.00 atm
B. 2.00 L of CO2 gas in a room at 25 °C and 1.00 atm
C. 2.00 L of 0.100 M HCl solution
D. 2.00 L of liquid water at 0 °C and 1.00 atm

Answer

A. 22.4 L/mol is the molar volume of an ideal gas at STP, 0 °C (273 K) and 1 atm. Any gas works, but only at those conditions: 2.00 L ÷ 22.4 L/mol = 0.0893 mol N2.

  • B is a gas, but not at STP. At 25 °C a mole of gas takes up more room (about 24.5 L), so 22.4 L/mol would overcount the moles. Gases at other conditions need the gas laws of Chapter 10.
  • C is a solution, not a gas. Use molarity: 2.00 L × 0.100 mol/L = 0.200 mol HCl.
  • D is a liquid. Molar volume applies only to gases; 2.00 L of water is about 2.00 × 103 g, or 111 mol.

Problem 8.21

Limewater, a dilute solution of calcium hydroxide, is used to test for CO2 in the lab. What volume of 0.100 M HCl, in milliliters, neutralizes 25.0 mL of 0.0200 M Ca(OH)2?

Ca(OH)2(aq) + 2 HCl(aq) → CaCl2(aq) + 2 H2O(l)

Answer

\( 0.0250\ \text{L} \times \dfrac{0.0200\ \text{mol Ca(OH)}_2}{1\ \text{L}} \times \dfrac{2\ \text{mol HCl}}{1\ \text{mol Ca(OH)}_2} \times \dfrac{1\ \text{L}}{0.100\ \text{mol HCl}} = 0.0100\ \text{L} \)

The answer is 10.0 mL of HCl. Moles of Ca(OH)2: 0.000500 mol; moles of HCl needed: 0.00100 mol, twice as many, because each Ca(OH)2 has two OH− ions.

Problem 8.22

A snack provides 4.50 g of glucose. (a) What volume of O2, measured at STP, do your cells need to oxidize it completely? (b) What volume of CO2 at STP do you exhale from it? (C6H12O6 180.156 g/mol)

C6H12O6(aq) + 6 O2(g) → 6 CO2(g) + 6 H2O(l)

Answer

(a) \( 4.50\ \text{g C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 \times \dfrac{1\ \text{mol C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6}{180.156\ \text{g}} \times \dfrac{6\ \text{mol O}_2}{1\ \text{mol C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6} \times \dfrac{22.4\ \text{L}}{1\ \text{mol O}_2} = 3.36\ \text{L O}_2 \)

(b) CO2 also has a coefficient of 6, so the same moles, 0.1499 mol, form, and at STP the same moles of any gas fill the same volume: 3.36 L of CO2.

(a) 3.36 L O2. Equal volumes of O2 used and CO2 made give a respiratory quotient of 1.0, the value for burning pure carbohydrate (Section 8.1).

Problem 8.23

A water-quality lab tests for chloride by adding silver nitrate. What mass of silver chloride precipitates from 20.0 mL of 0.150 M CaCl2 with excess AgNO3? (AgCl 143.32 g/mol)

CaCl2(aq) + 2 AgNO3(aq) → 2 AgCl(s) + Ca(NO3)2(aq)

A. 0.860 g
B. 0.430 g
C. 860. g
D. 0.215 g

Answer

A.

\( 0.0200\ \text{L} \times \dfrac{0.150\ \text{mol CaCl}_2}{1\ \text{L}} \times \dfrac{2\ \text{mol AgCl}}{1\ \text{mol CaCl}_2} \times \dfrac{143.32\ \text{g AgCl}}{1\ \text{mol AgCl}} = 0.860\ \text{g AgCl} \)

  • B leaves out the 2 : 1 mole ratio. Each CaCl2 carries two Cl− ions, so it makes two AgCl.
  • C multiplies the molarity by 20.0 mL instead of 0.0200 L, a thousand times too much. Molarity is moles per liter.
  • D uses the flipped ratio, 1 mol AgCl / 2 mol CaCl2.

Problem 8.24

A fertilizer plant makes ammonia from nitrogen and hydrogen, all measured at STP:

N2(g) + 3 H2(g) → 2 NH3(g)

To make 5.00 L of NH3, (a) what volume of H2 and (b) what volume of N2 are needed? (c) What mass of NH3 is 5.00 L at STP? (NH3 17.031 g/mol)

Answer

At the same temperature and pressure, every gas has the same molar volume, so the mole ratio is also a volume ratio. The 22.4 L/mol cancels out.

  • (a) 5.00 L NH3 × (3 L H2 / 2 L NH3) = 7.50 L H2
  • (b) 5.00 L NH3 × (1 L N2 / 2 L NH3) = 2.50 L N2

(c) \( 5.00\ \text{L NH}_3 \times \dfrac{1\ \text{mol NH}_3}{22.4\ \text{L}} \times \dfrac{17.031\ \text{g NH}_3}{1\ \text{mol NH}_3} = 3.80\ \text{g NH}_3 \)

(c) 3.80 g NH3. Notice 10.0 L of gas goes in and only 5.00 L comes out: volume, like moles of molecules, is not conserved, even though mass is.

8.4 Limiting Reactant

Problem 8.25

A mixture of 10.0 g of H2 and 10.0 g of O2 is ignited: 2 H2(g) + O2(g) → 2 H2O(l). Which is the limiting reactant? (H2 2.016 g/mol; O2 31.998 g/mol)

A. H2, because each H2 molecule is much lighter than an O2 molecule.
B. O2, because it can make only 0.625 mol of H2O, while the H2 could make 4.96 mol.
C. Neither, because equal masses of the two reactants are present.
D. H2, because the equation needs twice as much H2 as O2.

Answer

B. Run each reactant to the same product:

  • From H2: 10.0 g ÷ 2.016 g/mol = 4.960 mol H2 × (2 mol H2O / 2 mol H2) = 4.96 mol H2O.
  • From O2: 10.0 g ÷ 31.998 g/mol = 0.3125 mol O2 × (2 mol H2O / 1 mol O2) = 0.625 mol H2O.

O2 makes less water, so it runs out first. Only 0.625 mol (11.3 g) of water forms, and 10.0 − 1.26 = 8.74 g of H2 is left over.

  • A gets the logic backward: because H2 molecules are so light, 10.0 g of H2 is a huge number of moles, far more than the O2 can use.

Problem 8.42

A sports-medicine hot pack contains calcium chloride and a pouch of water. When the pouch breaks, the CaCl2 dissolves and the pack warms up. Which statement is correct?

A. The process is exothermic: ΔH is negative, and heat flows from the pack into the skin.
B. The process is endothermic: ΔH is positive, and heat flows from the pack into the skin.
C. The process is exothermic: ΔH is positive, because heat is produced.
D. The process is endothermic: ΔH is negative, and heat flows from the skin into the pack.

Answer

A. The pack gets warm, so the process releases heat to its surroundings (the pack's contents, then your skin). Heat released means exothermic and a negative ΔH.

  • B pairs "endothermic" with warming. An endothermic process absorbs heat and feels cold, like an instant cold pack.
  • C gets the sign backward. ΔH is the change in the system's enthalpy; when the system gives heat away, its enthalpy goes down, so ΔH is negative.

Calcium phosphate, the main mineral in bone and tooth enamel, precipitates when aqueous calcium chloride is mixed with aqueous sodium phosphate. A student mixes solutions containing 1.11 g of calcium chloride and 1.64 g of sodium phosphate and collects 0.952 g of dry calcium phosphate.

(a) Write the formulas of the three named compounds (Chapter 4). (b) Write the balanced equation; the other product is aqueous sodium chloride. (c) Which reactant is limiting? (d) Find the theoretical yield and (e) the percent yield. (CaCl2 110.98 g/mol; Na3PO4 163.94 g/mol; Ca3(PO4)2 310.17 g/mol)

Answer

(a) Ca2+ with Cl−: CaCl2. Na+ with PO43−: Na3PO4. Ca2+ with PO43−: 3 Ca2+ (6+) balance 2 PO43− (6−), Ca3(PO4)2.

(b) Balance Ca (3) and PO4 (2) first, then Na and Cl (6 each):

3 CaCl2(aq) + 2 Na3PO4(aq) → Ca3(PO4)2(s) + 6 NaCl(aq)

(c) Moles of Ca3(PO4)2 each reactant could make:

  • From CaCl2: 1.11 g ÷ 110.98 g/mol = 0.01000 mol × (1/3) = 0.003334 mol
  • From Na3PO4: 1.64 g ÷ 163.94 g/mol = 0.01000 mol × (1/2) = 0.005002 mol

The reactants are present in equal moles, but the recipe needs 3 CaCl2 for every 2 Na3PO4, so CaCl2 is limiting.

(d) \( 0.003334\ \text{mol} \times \dfrac{310.17\ \text{g Ca}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2}{1\ \text{mol}} = 1.034\ \text{g} \rightarrow 1.03\ \text{g} \)

(e) \( \dfrac{0.952\ \text{g}}{1.034\ \text{g}} \times 100\% = 92.1\% \)

(d) 1.03 g; (e) 92.1%. Carry the extra digit (1.034 g) into the percent-yield step and round once at the end; dividing by the rounded 1.03 g gives 92.4%.

Problem 8.51

A camping stove is tested with 22.0 g of propane, C3H8, and only 64.0 g of O2.

(a) Write the balanced equation for the complete combustion of propane. (b) Which reactant is limiting? (c) What mass of CO2 forms? (d) How much heat is released? (e) What mass of the excess reactant is left? (C3H8 44.097 g/mol; O2 31.998 g/mol; CO2 44.009 g/mol; ΔH = −2.220 × 103 kJ for the equation as written in part (a))

Answer

(a) Complete combustion makes CO2 and H2O. Balance C (3), then H (8, so 4 H2O), then O (6 + 4 = 10, so 5 O2):

C3H8(g) + 5 O2(g) → 3 CO2(g) + 4 H2O(l)

(b) Moles of CO2 each reactant could make:

  • From C3H8: 22.0 g ÷ 44.097 g/mol = 0.4989 mol × (3/1) = 1.497 mol CO2
  • From O2: 64.0 g ÷ 31.998 g/mol = 2.000 mol × (3/5) = 1.200 mol CO2

O2 is limiting, even though its mass is almost three times the propane's mass.

(c) 1.200 mol CO2 × 44.009 g/mol = 52.8 g CO2.

(d) \( 2.000\ \text{mol O}_2 \times \dfrac{2.220 \times 10^{3}\ \text{kJ released}}{5\ \text{mol O}_2} = 888\ \text{kJ released} \)

The heat must come from the limiting reactant too: the 0.4989 mol of propane could release 1.11 × 103 kJ, but only 888 kJ is released because the oxygen runs out.

(e) Propane used: 2.000 mol O2 × (1/5) = 0.4000 mol × 44.097 g/mol = 17.64 g. Left: 22.0 − 17.64 = 4.4 g C3H8. In a real stove, too little oxygen also leads to incomplete combustion and carbon monoxide.

Problem 8.52

Ammonium sulfate, a fertilizer used on Valley orchards, is made from ammonia and sulfuric acid. A plant test run uses 34.0 g of NH3 and 49.0 g of H2SO4 and collects 61.5 g of product.

(a) Write the formula of ammonium sulfate and the balanced equation (it is the only product). (b) Which reactant is limiting? (c) Find the theoretical yield and (d) the percent yield. (e) What mass of the excess reactant is left? (NH3 17.031 g/mol; H2SO4 98.07 g/mol; (NH4)2SO4 132.13 g/mol)

Answer

(a) Two NH4+ ions balance one SO42− ion: (NH4)2SO4. Each NH3 picks up one H+ from the acid:

2 NH3(g) + H2SO4(aq) → (NH4)2SO4(aq)

(b) Moles of (NH4)2SO4 each reactant could make:

  • From NH3: 34.0 g ÷ 17.031 g/mol = 1.996 mol × (1/2) = 0.9982 mol
  • From H2SO4: 49.0 g ÷ 98.07 g/mol = 0.4996 mol × (1/1) = 0.4996 mol

H2SO4 is limiting, even though it has the larger mass.

(c) \( 0.4996\ \text{mol} \times \dfrac{132.13\ \text{g (NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4}{1\ \text{mol}} = 66.0\ \text{g} \)

(d) \( \dfrac{61.5\ \text{g}}{66.0\ \text{g}} \times 100\% = 93.2\% \)

(e) NH3 used: 0.4996 mol H2SO4 × 2 = 0.9993 mol × 17.031 g/mol = 17.02 g. Left: 34.0 − 17.02 = 17.0 g NH3, half of what was loaded.

(c) 66.0 g; (d) 93.2%.

Problem 8.53

A disposable hand warmer heats up as iron powder rusts to iron(III) oxide in air. The pack contains 20.0 g of iron.

(a) Write the formula of iron(III) oxide and the balanced equation for iron reacting with O2. (b) How much heat can the pack release? (c) What volume of O2, at STP, must it take in from the air? (Fe 55.85 g/mol; ΔH = −1648 kJ for the equation as balanced with whole-number coefficients)

Answer

(a) Fe3+ and O2− combine 2 : 3: Fe2O3. Balance O first (3 O2 → 2 Fe2O3, 6 O each side), then Fe (4):

4 Fe(s) + 3 O2(g) → 2 Fe2O3(s)    ΔH = −1648 kJ

(b) \( 20.0\ \text{g Fe} \times \dfrac{1\ \text{mol Fe}}{55.85\ \text{g}} \times \dfrac{1648\ \text{kJ released}}{4\ \text{mol Fe}} = 148\ \text{kJ released} \)

(c) \( 0.3581\ \text{mol Fe} \times \dfrac{3\ \text{mol O}_2}{4\ \text{mol Fe}} \times \dfrac{22.4\ \text{L}}{1\ \text{mol O}_2} = 6.02\ \text{L O}_2 \)

(b) 148 kJ released, spread over several hours by the slow rusting; (c) 6.02 L of O2, which is why the pack works only after you open its airtight wrapper. The ΔH belongs to 4 mol of Fe, so the ratio is 1648 kJ / 4 mol Fe.

Problem 8.54

A Valley winery ferments grape juice containing 225 g of glucose: C6H12O6(aq) → 2 C2H5OH(aq) + 2 CO2(g).

(a) What is the theoretical yield of ethanol? (b) What volume of CO2, at STP, is released? (c) If 98.0 g of ethanol is actually produced, what is the percent yield? (d) Give one biological reason the yield is below 100%. (C6H12O6 180.156 g/mol; C2H5OH 46.069 g/mol)

Answer

Moles of glucose: 225 g ÷ 180.156 g/mol = 1.249 mol.

(a) \( 1.249\ \text{mol C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 \times \dfrac{2\ \text{mol C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}}{1\ \text{mol C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6} \times \dfrac{46.069\ \text{g}}{1\ \text{mol C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}} = 115\ \text{g C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} \)

(b) \( 1.249\ \text{mol C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 \times \dfrac{2\ \text{mol CO}_2}{1\ \text{mol C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6} \times \dfrac{22.4\ \text{L}}{1\ \text{mol CO}_2} = 56.0\ \text{L CO}_2 \)

(c) \( \dfrac{98.0\ \text{g}}{115\ \text{g}} \times 100\% = 85.2\% \)

(a) 115 g of ethanol; (b) 56.0 L of CO2; (c) 85.2%.

(d) The yeast are alive: they use some of the glucose to grow and build new cells, and they turn some into other products (such as glycerol), so not every glucose molecule becomes ethanol. Some ethanol also evaporates with the escaping CO2.

Problem 8.55

The table below lists the active ingredient in one tablet of three antacids. Each reacts with stomach acid, HCl.

Active ingredients in three antacid tablets (blanks marked ?)
Active ingredientFormulaMass per tablet (mg)Molar mass (g/mol)mmol HCl neutralized
Calcium carbonate?750.100.09?
Magnesium hydroxide?400.58.32?
Sodium hydrogen carbonate?600.84.01?

(a) Write each formula and a balanced equation for its reaction with HCl. (The carbonates make a chloride salt, water, and CO2; the hydroxide makes a chloride salt and water.) (b) Fill in the mmol of HCl each tablet neutralizes. (c) Which tablet neutralizes the most acid, and which ingredient neutralizes the most acid per gram?

Answer

(a) The formulas are CaCO3, Mg(OH)2, and NaHCO3:

CaCO3(s) + 2 HCl(aq) → CaCl2(aq) + H2O(l) + CO2(g)

Mg(OH)2(s) + 2 HCl(aq) → MgCl2(aq) + 2 H2O(l)

NaHCO3(s) + HCl(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H2O(l) + CO2(g)

(b) Milligrams divided by g/mol (the same number as mg/mmol) give millimoles; then use the mole ratio:

  • CaCO3: 750. mg ÷ 100.09 mg/mmol = 7.493 mmol × 2 = 15.0 mmol HCl
  • Mg(OH)2: 400. mg ÷ 58.32 mg/mmol = 6.859 mmol × 2 = 13.7 mmol HCl
  • NaHCO3: 600. mg ÷ 84.01 mg/mmol = 7.142 mmol × 1 = 7.14 mmol HCl

(c) The calcium carbonate tablet neutralizes the most acid, mostly because it contains the most active ingredient. Per gram, magnesium hydroxide wins: 13.7 mmol ÷ 0.400 g = 34.3 mmol/g, versus 20.0 mmol/g for CaCO3 and 11.9 mmol/g for NaHCO3. Its small molar mass and its 2 : 1 ratio with HCl both help.

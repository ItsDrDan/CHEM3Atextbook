Learning Objectives

  • Interpret the coefficients of a balanced equation as mole ratios.
  • Write the mole ratios between any two substances in a reaction.
  • Convert moles of one substance into moles of another.

A recipe that says "1 frame + 2 wheels makes 1 bicycle" lets you plan: 10 bicycles need 10 frames and 20 wheels. A balanced chemical equation works exactly the same way, except the "parts" are molecules, and the ratios hold whether you count single molecules, dozens, or moles.

Coefficients Are Mole Ratios

In the balanced equation for making ammonia,

N2(g) + 3 H2(g) → 2 NH3(g)

the coefficients say that 1 molecule of N2 reacts with 3 molecules of H2 to make 2 molecules of NH3. Multiply every number by Avogadro's number and the same statement holds for moles: 1 mol N2 : 3 mol H2 : 2 mol NH3. Any pair gives a mole ratio, a conversion factor that can be written either way up:

\( \dfrac{3\ \text{mol H}_2}{1\ \text{mol N}_2} \qquad \dfrac{2\ \text{mol NH}_3}{3\ \text{mol H}_2} \qquad \dfrac{1\ \text{mol N}_2}{2\ \text{mol NH}_3} \)

Mole ratios come only from a balanced equation, and they are exact, so they never limit significant figures. Coefficients do not give mass ratios: 1 g of N2 does not react with 3 g of H2, because the molecules have different masses.

Example \(\PageIndex{1}\): Mole-to-mole

Problem. How many moles of NH3 can be made from 4.20 mol of H2 (with plenty of N2)?

Solution.

\( 4.20\ \text{mol H}_2 \times \dfrac{2\ \text{mol NH}_3}{3\ \text{mol H}_2} = 2.80\ \text{mol NH}_3 \)

Check. The equation makes fewer moles of NH3 than it uses of H2 (2 for every 3), so the answer should be smaller than 4.20.

Example \(\PageIndex{2}\): Cellular respiration

Problem. Using C6H12O6 + 6 O2 → 6 CO2 + 6 H2O, how many moles of O2 are needed, and how many moles of CO2 are made, when 0.150 mol of glucose is used?

Solution. 0.150 mol glucose × (6 mol O2 / 1 mol glucose) = 0.900 mol O2. The ratio of CO2 to glucose is also 6 : 1, so 0.900 mol CO2 forms.

Check. For glucose, moles of CO2 made equal moles of O2 used (6 and 6).

Professor Tip

Write the mole ratio with the unit you want on top and the unit you have on the bottom, and include the formulas, not just "mol." "2 mol NH3 / 3 mol H2" cancels mol H2 and leaves mol NH3. If you ever write a mole ratio from an unbalanced equation, every answer after it will be wrong, so balance first, every time.

Clinical Connection: Respiratory Therapy

The ratio of CO2 produced to O2 consumed is called the respiratory quotient (RQ). For burning pure glucose, the equation gives 6 mol CO2 / 6 mol O2 = 1.0. Fats contain much less oxygen and need more O2 for each CO2 made, so their RQ is about 0.7. A typical mixed diet gives an RQ near 0.8: an adult at rest uses roughly 250 mL of O2 per minute and produces roughly 200 mL of CO2. Respiratory therapists and dietitians use indirect calorimetry, measuring the O2 in and CO2 out of a patient's breath, to estimate how many calories a critically ill patient is burning. It is stoichiometry at the bedside.

Common Mistakes

  • Using an unbalanced equation. Mole ratios are only correct for a balanced equation.
  • Flipping the ratio. Put the substance you want on top.
  • Treating coefficients as grams. They are ratios of particles and moles, not masses.

Summary

  • Coefficients in a balanced equation give mole ratios between any two substances.
  • moles of A × (mol B / mol A) = moles of B.
  • Mole ratios are exact and come only from balanced equations.

Exercises

Exercise \(\PageIndex{1}\)

For 4 Fe(s) + 3 O2(g) → 2 Fe2O3(s), how many moles of O2 react with 2.0 mol Fe?

Answer

2.0 mol Fe × 3 mol O2 / 4 mol Fe = 1.5 mol O2.

Exercise \(\PageIndex{2}\)

For C3H8 + 5 O2 → 3 CO2 + 4 H2O, how many moles of water form from 0.75 mol propane?

Answer

0.75 × 4/1 = 3.0 mol H2O.

Exercise \(\PageIndex{3}\)

Write all six mole ratios for 2 H2O2 → 2 H2O + O2.

Answer

2 mol H2O2/2 mol H2O and its inverse; 2 mol H2O2/1 mol O2 and its inverse; 2 mol H2O/1 mol O2 and its inverse.

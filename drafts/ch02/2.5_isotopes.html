Learning Objectives

  • Define isotopes and mass number.
  • Read and write isotope notation in both forms (symbol with A and Z, and name-mass number).
  • Count the protons, neutrons, and electrons in an isotope or an isotope ion.
  • Explain why isotopes of an element behave the same way chemically.

Dalton thought every atom of an element had the same mass. He was close, but not quite right. Atoms of the same element always have the same number of protons, but they can have different numbers of neutrons. Those variations are isotopes, and they matter far beyond the chemistry classroom: they date ancient bones, trace drugs through the body, and light up tumors in a scan.

Mass Number and Isotopes

The mass number, symbol A, is the total number of protons and neutrons in an atom's nucleus:

mass number (A) = number of protons (Z) + number of neutrons

number of neutrons = A − Z

Because protons and neutrons each have a mass of about 1 amu, the mass number is close to the atom's mass in amu. Electrons are left out of the count because they are so light.

Isotopes are atoms of the same element (same Z) with different numbers of neutrons (different A). Hydrogen has three (Figure \(\PageIndex{1}\)): hydrogen-1 with no neutrons, hydrogen-2 (deuterium) with one, and hydrogen-3 (tritium) with two. All three have one proton, so all three are hydrogen.

Writing Isotopes

There are two common ways to write an isotope.

  • Isotope symbol: the mass number as a superscript and the atomic number as a subscript, both on the left of the element symbol: \( ^{37}_{17}\text{Cl} \). Because the symbol already tells you Z, the subscript is sometimes left off: \( ^{37}\text{Cl} \).
  • Name or symbol with a hyphen: chlorine-37, or Cl-37. The number is the mass number.

If the isotope is also an ion, the charge goes at the upper right as usual: \( ^{37}_{17}\text{Cl}^{-} \).

Professor Tip

Fill in a small table every time: symbol, Z, A, protons, neutrons, electrons, charge. Z = protons always. Neutrons = A − Z always. Electrons = protons − charge. Three rules, and any blank in the table can be filled in.

Example \(\PageIndex{1}\): Count the particles in isotopes

Problem. Complete the table for each atom or ion.

Isotopes to complete (blanks marked ?)
IsotopeZAProtonsNeutronsElectrons
Cr-53?????
\( ^{18}_{8}\text{O}^{2-} \)?????
a carbon atom with 8 neutrons?????

Solution.

  • Cr-53: chromium has Z = 24, so 24 protons; A = 53, so neutrons = 53 − 24 = 29; neutral, so 24 electrons.
  • \( ^{18}_{8}\text{O}^{2-} \): Z = 8, A = 18, 8 protons, 18 − 8 = 10 neutrons, and the 2− charge means 8 + 2 = 10 electrons.
  • Carbon with 8 neutrons: Z = 6, so 6 protons; A = 6 + 8 = 14 (carbon-14, the isotope used in radiocarbon dating); neutral, so 6 electrons.

Check. For each row, protons + neutrons = A, and protons − electrons = charge (0, 2−, 0).

Same Chemistry, Different Mass

Chemical behavior depends on electrons, and the number of electrons depends on the number of protons. Isotopes of an element have the same number of protons and (as neutral atoms) the same number of electrons, so they react in essentially the same way. They differ in mass, and some isotopes have unstable nuclei that break apart, giving off radiation. Those are radioactive isotopes (radioisotopes), which you will study in Chapter 14.

Example \(\PageIndex{2}\): Same element or not?

Problem. Which two of these are isotopes of the same element: Atom 1 (20 protons, 20 neutrons), Atom 2 (19 protons, 21 neutrons), Atom 3 (20 protons, 24 neutrons)?

Solution. Atoms 1 and 3 both have 20 protons, so both are calcium; they are isotopes (calcium-40 and calcium-44). Atom 2 has 19 protons, so it is potassium-40, a different element, even though its mass number (40) matches Atom 1.

Check. Isotopes share Z, not A. Atoms 1 and 2 have the same mass number but are different elements.

Clinical Connection: Radiologic Technology

Nuclear medicine depends on the fact that isotopes of an element behave alike chemically. The thyroid gland takes up iodine to make its hormones, and it cannot tell stable iodine-127 from radioactive iodine-131 or iodine-123. So a small dose of a radioactive iodine isotope goes straight to the thyroid, where its radiation can be imaged or, at higher doses ordered by a physician, can destroy overactive thyroid tissue. The most widely used isotope in nuclear medicine imaging, technetium-99m, is attached to different carrier molecules so that it travels to the bone, heart, or lungs. The technologist's job includes handling these isotopes safely: time, distance, and shielding.

Biology Connection: Microbiology

Isotopes also help diagnose infections. The bacterium Helicobacter pylori, a common cause of stomach ulcers, makes an enzyme called urease that breaks urea down into ammonia and carbon dioxide. In the urea breath test, a patient drinks urea made with carbon-13, a stable, non-radioactive isotope of carbon with one more neutron than ordinary carbon-12. If H. pylori is present, its urease releases the carbon-13 as CO2, which enters the blood and is breathed out. An instrument that sorts molecules by mass detects the extra carbon-13 in the breath sample. Without the bacteria, there is no extra heavy CO2. The test works because carbon-13 behaves chemically like carbon-12 but can be told apart by its mass.

Common Mistakes

  • Using the atomic mass from the periodic table as the mass number. The mass number is a whole number for one isotope; the atomic mass is a decimal average (Section 2.6).
  • Thinking isotopes are different elements. Same protons, same element; only neutrons differ.
  • Subtracting the charge when counting neutrons. Charge affects electrons only; neutrons = A − Z.

Summary

  • Mass number A = protons + neutrons; neutrons = A − Z.
  • Isotopes have the same number of protons and different numbers of neutrons.
  • Isotopes are written \( ^{A}_{Z}\text{X} \) or as name-A (chlorine-37).
  • Isotopes of an element behave alike chemically; some are radioactive.

Exercises

Exercise \(\PageIndex{1}\)

How many protons, neutrons, and electrons are in iodine-131?

Answer

I has Z = 53: 53 protons, 131 − 53 = 78 neutrons, 53 electrons (neutral).

Exercise \(\PageIndex{2}\)

Write the isotope symbol for the atom with 26 protons and 30 neutrons.

Answer

Z = 26 is iron; A = 26 + 30 = 56: \( ^{56}_{26}\text{Fe} \) (iron-56).

Exercise \(\PageIndex{3}\)

How many electrons are in \( ^{24}_{12}\text{Mg}^{2+} \)?

Answer

12 protons; charge 2+, so 12 − 2 = 10 electrons. (It has 12 neutrons.)

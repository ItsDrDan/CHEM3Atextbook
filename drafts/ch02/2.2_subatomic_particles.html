Learning Objectives

  • Give the charge, approximate mass, and location of a proton, a neutron, and an electron.
  • Use the atomic number (Z) to identify an element and to count its protons.
  • Find the number of electrons in a neutral atom.
  • Define the atomic mass unit (amu).

Every atom of every element is built from just three particles. What makes gold different from oxygen, and oxygen different from sodium, is only how many of each particle the atom has. Learning to count them is the first real skill of chemistry, and you will use it in every chapter that follows.

The Three Subatomic Particles

The table below compares the three subatomic particles. Because real particle masses in grams are awkwardly small, chemists use the atomic mass unit (amu), defined as one-twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom: 1 amu = 1.6605 × 10−24 g. On that scale, protons and neutrons each have a mass of about 1 amu, and an electron is roughly 1800 times lighter.

The table above shows two patterns worth remembering. First, the proton and electron have charges of exactly equal size but opposite sign. Second, nearly all of an atom's mass is in its nucleus, because protons and neutrons are so much heavier than electrons. The nucleus is also incredibly small: its diameter is roughly 1/100,000 of the atom's diameter.

Atomic Number: Protons Define the Element

The number of protons in an atom's nucleus is its atomic number, symbol Z. The atomic number is what makes an element that element: every atom with 6 protons is carbon, every atom with 8 protons is oxygen, and every atom with 79 protons is gold. Change the number of protons and you have a different element. On the periodic table, the atomic number is the whole number in each box, and the elements are arranged in order of increasing Z.

Neutral atoms

An atom has no overall charge, so its positive and negative charges must cancel. Since a proton and an electron carry equal and opposite charges, a neutral atom has equal numbers of protons and electrons:

number of electrons in a neutral atom = number of protons = Z

Neutrons do not affect charge. Their number can vary among atoms of the same element, as you will see in Section 2.5.

Professor Tip

The periodic table gives you the atomic number for free, and the atomic number gives you the protons. For a neutral atom it also gives you the electrons. So for any neutral element, two of the three counts come straight off the table. Only neutrons need more information (the mass number, Section 2.5).

Example \(\PageIndex{1}\): Protons and electrons in neutral atoms

Problem. Using the periodic table, find the number of protons and electrons in a neutral atom of (a) potassium, K; (b) iodine, I; (c) iron, Fe.

Solution. Read each atomic number, then set electrons equal to protons. (a) K: Z = 19, so 19 protons and 19 electrons. (b) I: Z = 53, so 53 protons and 53 electrons. (c) Fe: Z = 26, so 26 protons and 26 electrons.

Check. In each case protons = electrons, so the charges cancel and the atom is neutral.

Example \(\PageIndex{2}\): Identify the element

Problem. A neutral atom has 20 electrons. What element is it, and how many protons does it have?

Solution. A neutral atom has as many protons as electrons, so it has 20 protons. The element with Z = 20 is calcium, Ca.

Check. The identity comes from the proton count, not the electron count; here they are equal because the atom is neutral.

Biology Connection: Anatomy and Physiology

About 60 elements can be detected in the human body, but only about two dozen are known to be needed. Some are needed in tiny amounts, the trace elements. Iron (Z = 26) sits at the center of each heme group in hemoglobin, the protein that carries oxygen in red blood cells. Iodine (Z = 53) is built into the thyroid hormones that set your metabolic rate. Zinc (Z = 30) helps many enzymes work. What makes each of these elements useful to the body is fixed by its atomic number: an iron atom behaves like iron because it has 26 protons and, in its neutral form, 26 electrons.

Common Mistakes

  • Using the atomic mass instead of the atomic number. The atomic number is the whole number; the decimal number is the average atomic mass (Section 2.6).
  • Thinking electrons define the element. Only the number of protons does; electrons can be gained or lost (Section 2.4).
  • Thinking neutrons carry charge. Neutrons are neutral; they add mass only.

Summary

  • Protons (1+, about 1 amu) and neutrons (0, about 1 amu) are in the nucleus; electrons (1−, about 0 amu) are outside it.
  • The atomic number Z is the number of protons and identifies the element.
  • A neutral atom has equal numbers of protons and electrons.

Exercises

Exercise \(\PageIndex{1}\)

How many protons and electrons are in a neutral atom of magnesium?

Answer

Mg has Z = 12: 12 protons and 12 electrons.

Exercise \(\PageIndex{2}\)

Which subatomic particle has (a) no charge, (b) almost no mass, (c) a positive charge?

Answer

(a) neutron; (b) electron; (c) proton.

Exercise \(\PageIndex{3}\)

An atom has 17 protons. What element is it, and how many electrons does it have if it is neutral?

Answer

Z = 17 is chlorine, Cl; a neutral chlorine atom has 17 electrons.

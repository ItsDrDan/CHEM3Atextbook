Learning Objectives

  • Explain why the atomic mass on the periodic table is a weighted average and is not a whole number.
  • Calculate an element's average atomic mass from the masses and percent abundances of its isotopes.
  • Predict which isotope of an element is most abundant from its average atomic mass.
  • Find a missing percent abundance by subtracting from 100%, and use it to calculate an average atomic mass.

Look at chlorine on the periodic table and you will see 35.45 under the symbol. No single chlorine atom has a mass of 35.45 amu. Natural chlorine is a mixture of two isotopes, chlorine-35 and chlorine-37, and 35.45 amu is their weighted average. Every time you use an atomic mass, whether to find a molar mass in Chapter 5 or to calculate a dose, you are using one of these averages.

Weighted Averages

A weighted average counts each value according to how often it occurs. Your course grade works this way: if exams are worth 60% and homework 40%, an exam score counts more. For an element, each isotope's mass is weighted by its percent abundance, the percentage of the element's atoms in nature that are that isotope.

\( \text{average atomic mass} = \sum (\text{fractional abundance} \times \text{isotope mass}) \)

The fractional abundance is the percent divided by 100 (75.77% becomes 0.7577). The fractional abundances of all the isotopes add up to 1.

A weighted average is not the same as the plain average (mean) you learned in math class. Imagine an element with three isotopes of mass 100.0, 101.0, and 102.0 amu. The plain average is (100.0 + 101.0 + 102.0) ÷ 3 = 101.0 amu, and it would be the right answer only if the three isotopes were equally common. Suppose instead that 50.0% of the atoms are the 100.0-amu isotope, 30.0% are the 101.0-amu isotope, and 20.0% are the 102.0-amu isotope. Now the lighter isotope counts most:

\( (0.500)(100.0\;\text{amu}) + (0.300)(101.0\;\text{amu}) + (0.200)(102.0\;\text{amu}) = 50.0 + 30.3 + 20.4 = 100.7\;\text{amu} \)

The weighted average, 100.7 amu, is pulled toward the most common isotope. If you did the bead lab, this is the same calculation: the bead "isotopes" of each color were weighed and counted, and the average mass was weighted by how many beads of each color were in the sample.

Example \(\PageIndex{1}\): Chlorine

Problem. Chlorine-35 has a mass of 34.97 amu and an abundance of 75.77%. Chlorine-37 has a mass of 36.97 amu and an abundance of 24.23%. Calculate the average atomic mass of chlorine.

Solution.

\( (0.7577)(34.97\;\text{amu}) + (0.2423)(36.97\;\text{amu}) = 26.496\;\text{amu} + 8.9578\;\text{amu} = 35.45\;\text{amu} \)

Check. The answer lies between 34.97 and 36.97 and is closer to 34.97, because chlorine-35 is the more abundant isotope. It matches the periodic table.

Example \(\PageIndex{2}\): Three isotopes

Problem. Magnesium has three natural isotopes, listed in the table below. Calculate magnesium's average atomic mass.

Natural isotopes of magnesium
IsotopeMass (amu)Abundance (%)
Mg-2423.98578.99
Mg-2524.98610.00
Mg-2625.98311.01

Solution. Convert each percent to a fraction, multiply, and add:

\( (0.7899)(23.985) + (0.1000)(24.986) + (0.1101)(25.983) = 18.946 + 2.4986 + 2.8607 = 24.31\;\text{amu} \)

Each product is limited to 4 significant figures by the abundance; carrying the full numbers and rounding once at the end gives 24.31 amu.

Check. The abundances add to 100.00%, and the answer is close to 24 because Mg-24 is by far the most common isotope. It matches the periodic table (24.305).

Example \(\PageIndex{3}\): A hypothetical element

Problem. A made-up element has two isotopes. Isotope A has a mass of 52.94 amu and an abundance of 35.20%. Isotope B has a mass of 54.93 amu and an abundance of 64.80%. Find the average atomic mass of the element.

Given: the masses and abundances in the problem. Desire: the average atomic mass.

Solution. Change the percents to decimals (0.3520 and 0.6480), multiply each by its mass, and add:

\( (0.3520)(52.94\;\text{amu}) + (0.6480)(54.93\;\text{amu}) = 18.63\;\text{amu} + 35.59\;\text{amu} = 54.23\;\text{amu} \)

Check. The answer lies between 52.94 and 54.93 and is closer to 54.93, because isotope B is almost two-thirds of the atoms. (Carrying all digits gives 54.2295, which also rounds to 54.23.)

Professor Tip

Before you calculate, predict. The average must fall between the lightest and heaviest isotope masses, and it will be closest to the most abundant isotope. If your answer is outside that range, you probably forgot to change a percent into a decimal (multiplying by 75.77 instead of 0.7577 gives an answer about 100 times too big). On the TI-36X Pro, type the whole sum on one line and press enter once, so you round only at the end. Always use parentheses around each fraction-times-mass pair, for example

(0.7577 × 34.97) + (0.2423 × 36.97)

so the order of operations cannot get crisscrossed. On a multiple-choice question, the prediction also lets you eliminate answers: any choice outside the range of the isotope masses is impossible, and the right answer sits closest to the isotope with the biggest percentage.

Example \(\PageIndex{4}\): Which isotope is more common?

Problem. Copper has two natural isotopes, copper-63 and copper-65. Its average atomic mass is 63.55 amu. Which isotope is more abundant?

Solution. 63.55 is much closer to 63 than to 65, so copper-63 must make up most of natural copper. (It is about 69%.)

Check. If the two isotopes were equally common, the average would be about 64; the real average is below 64, so the lighter isotope wins.

When One Abundance Is Missing

Some problems give you only one of two percent abundances and ask for the other, or ask for the average anyway. Students often overthink these and reach for a big equation. Read the problem carefully first. Every atom of the element is one isotope or another, so the percent abundances always add up to 100%. With two isotopes, the missing one is just

percent of isotope 2 = 100% − percent of isotope 1

With three isotopes, subtract both known percents from 100%. This problem type is extremely common on homework and exams.

Example \(\PageIndex{5}\): Find the missing abundance, then the average

Problem. Natural copper is made of copper-63 (62.930 amu) and copper-65 (64.928 amu). Copper-63 makes up 69.15% of copper atoms. (a) What is the percent abundance of copper-65? (b) Calculate the average atomic mass of copper.

Given: both masses and one abundance. Desire: the other abundance, then the average.

Solution. (a) The two percents must total 100%:

\( 100.00\% - 69.15\% = 30.85\%\;\text{copper-65} \)

(b) Now both abundances are known. Change them to decimals and use the weighted average:

\( (0.6915)(62.930\;\text{amu}) + (0.3085)(64.928\;\text{amu}) = 43.52\;\text{amu} + 20.03\;\text{amu} = 63.55\;\text{amu} \)

Check. 69.15% + 30.85% = 100.00%. The average, 63.55 amu, is between the two masses and closer to copper-63, the larger share, and it matches the 63.55 on the yellow-sheet periodic table.

Clinical Connection: Nursing

Instruments that sort particles by mass, called mass spectrometers, are how isotope abundances are measured, and they are also everyday tools in hospital and public-health laboratories. California's Newborn Screening Program, for example, uses a form of mass spectrometry to test a few drops of blood from a heel stick for dozens of rare but treatable metabolic disorders in the first days of life. For a nurse or a family, the result is a simple "screen negative" or a call to follow up; underneath it is the same idea you just used: particles that differ in mass can be separated and counted.

Common Mistakes

  • Using percents instead of decimals. Divide each percent by 100 first.
  • Taking a simple average. (34.97 + 36.97) ÷ 2 = 35.97 ignores abundance; the weighted answer is 35.45.
  • Rounding each product too early. Round once, at the end.
  • Overthinking a missing abundance. The percents add to 100%, so the missing one is 100% minus the others; no equation solving is needed.
  • Leaving out parentheses on the calculator. Put each fraction × mass pair in its own parentheses.
  • Thinking any one atom has the average mass. The average describes the mixture, not a single atom.

Summary

  • The atomic mass on the periodic table is the weighted average of the masses of an element's natural isotopes.
  • Average atomic mass = Σ (fractional abundance × isotope mass), with fractional abundance = percent ÷ 100.
  • The average lies between the isotope masses, closest to the most abundant isotope; it equals the plain mean only if the isotopes are equally common.
  • Percent abundances add up to 100%, so a missing abundance is 100% minus the known ones.

Exercises

Exercise \(\PageIndex{1}\)

Bromine is 50.69% bromine-79 (78.918 amu) and 49.31% bromine-81 (80.916 amu). Calculate its average atomic mass.

Hint

Change each percent to a decimal, multiply by that isotope's mass, and add. Predict first: the two isotopes are nearly equally abundant, so where should the answer fall?

Answer

(0.5069)(78.918) + (0.4931)(80.916) = 40.004 + 39.900 = 79.90 amu. It sits almost exactly halfway between, because the two isotopes are nearly equally abundant.

Exercise \(\PageIndex{2}\)

Lithium has two isotopes, lithium-6 and lithium-7, and an average atomic mass of 6.94 amu. Which is more abundant?

Hint

Compare the average with the two mass numbers. A weighted average is pulled toward the more abundant isotope.

Answer

Lithium-7: 6.94 is much closer to 7 than to 6.

A weighted average sits closest to the most abundant isotope, and 6.94 is much closer to 7 than to 6.

Exercise \(\PageIndex{3}\)

Gallium is 60.11% gallium-69 (68.926 amu) and 39.89% gallium-71 (70.925 amu). Calculate its average atomic mass.

Hint

Use (fraction × mass) + (fraction × mass), dividing each percent by 100. The answer must land between the two masses, closer to the more abundant isotope.

Answer

(0.6011)(68.926) + (0.3989)(70.925) = 41.431 + 28.292 = 69.72 amu.

Exercise \(\PageIndex{4}\)

Rubidium has two natural isotopes. Rubidium-85 (84.912 amu) makes up 72.17% of rubidium atoms; the rest is rubidium-87 (86.909 amu). (a) What percent is rubidium-87? (b) Calculate the average atomic mass.

Hint

(a) The two percents must add to 100%. (b) Change both percents to decimals, multiply each by its isotope mass, and add.

Answer

(a) 100.00% − 72.17% = 27.83%. (b) (0.7217)(84.912) + (0.2783)(86.909) = 61.28 + 24.19 = 85.47 amu, closer to 85 because rubidium-85 is the majority.

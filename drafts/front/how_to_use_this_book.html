This book is free, works on your phone, and was written for CHEM 3A students, most of whom are headed for careers in health care, science, or engineering. This page shows you what is on each page and how to use it so your study time counts.

What You Will Find in Each Chapter

  • Learning Objectives at the top of every section list what you should be able to do when you finish it. Use them as a checklist before a quiz.
  • Worked Examples show every step in three parts: Problem, Solution, and Check. Cover the solution, try the problem yourself, then compare. The Check step shows how to tell whether an answer makes sense, a habit that catches most mistakes.
  • Professor Tips and Common Mistakes are the advice you would hear in office hours: shortcuts that work, and the traps students fall into most often.
  • Connection boxes show where the chemistry shows up in nursing, dental hygiene, radiologic technology, respiratory therapy, biology, and the Central Valley. Several chapters follow one patient, Mateo, through his care.
  • Video Lessons are short lectures, with links that jump straight to the part you need.
  • Going Further pages and boxes go past what CHEM 3A tests. Read them if you are curious; your instructor will tell you if they are assigned.

How to Use the Practice

Exercises: try the Hint before the Answer

Every exercise and practice problem has two buttons: Hint and Answer. Work the problem on paper first. If you get stuck, open the Hint; it points to the first step without giving the answer away. Open the Answer only after you have an answer of your own, then read the reasoning, not just the final number. Opening the answer first feels faster but teaches much less.

Try It boxes

The Try It boxes inside the sections are quick, ungraded multiple-choice checks. Every choice, right or wrong, explains why, so a wrong answer is a chance to see the mistake. They need no login and keep your progress in your browser.

Explore It boxes

Explore It boxes hold interactive labs and simulations: build a Lewis structure, heat ice to steam, squeeze a gas, stress an equilibrium. Each one follows the same rule: predict what will happen, try it, then explain what you saw.

Practice Problems and Practice Games

Each chapter ends with a Practice Problems page (40 to 60 problems, every answer with its reasoning) and a Practice Games page with study games for the chapter, including the Final Boss exam-review game. Your instructor may also assign homework in ADAPT, which gives feedback on every answer choice and lets you use a hint without losing points.

Each Chapter Guide Is Your Study Sheet

Near the end of every chapter, the Chapter Guide collects the whole chapter on one page: Key Ideas, Key Equations (with the section that teaches each one), Key Terms (every term with its meaning), the video lessons, extra worked examples, and Common Mistakes. Start your exam review there, and go back to a section only for the parts that still feel shaky.

Your Reference Sheets

The numbers in this book match the course reference sheets you use on exams: the yellow sheet (periodic table, conversions, and constants) and the green sheet (polyatomic ions, solubility rules, and strong acids and bases). Practice with the same sheets you will have on the exam, so you know where everything is. Calculator steps are written for the TI-36X Pro.

A Weekly Study Routine That Works

  1. Before class: skim the section's Learning Objectives and its first worked example (10 minutes).
  2. After class, the same day: work the worked examples yourself, then the section's Try It box and two or three exercises, using Hints before Answers (30 to 45 minutes).
  3. Every few days: play a short round of the chapter's game or the Final Boss on earlier chapters. Short, spread-out practice beats one long session the night before.
  4. Before a quiz or exam: read the Chapter Guide, rework any example you could not do on your own, and do a set of Practice Problems without opening the answers until you finish.

Near the end of the term, use Preparing for the ACS Final in the back of the book.

Other Ways to Find Things

  • Words with a dotted underline open a short definition when you tap or hover over them. The same definitions are in the Glossary at the back of the book, and the Index lists where each topic is taught.
  • The search box at the top of every page searches the whole book.
  • The book can be downloaded as a PDF from the menu at the top of any page, if you prefer to study on paper. Hints and answers print open.
  • If something is hard to read or use with a screen reader, keyboard, or phone, see the Accessibility Statement and let your instructor know.

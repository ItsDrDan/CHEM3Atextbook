This book is built to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 at level AA, the standard California community colleges follow.

What the book does

  • Every figure has a text description for screen readers and a visible caption, and the pictures inside practice questions (graduated cylinders, burets, rulers, balances, dart targets) describe what a sighted reader sees, such as where the meniscus sits between two marks.
  • Tables have header cells and a sentence that introduces them; on a phone they reflow into labeled cards.
  • Headings follow one outline on every page, formulas are written as text rather than pictures, and meaning is never carried by color alone.
  • Links say where they go, and hidden answers use a standard disclosure control that works with the keyboard and with screen readers.
  • The Try It practice boxes use ordinary buttons, announce right-and-wrong feedback to screen readers, show a visible focus ring, keep keyboard focus in the box after you check an answer, and meet 4.5:1 text contrast and 44-pixel touch targets. Each one has an “Open this practice in a new tab” link for a larger view.
  • The games linked from the Practice Games pages are optional practice and have no required timed mode.

How it was checked

Every page of the book was scanned with an automated WCAG 2.1 A/AA tool (axe-core) at phone width with all hidden answers opened, every external link and video was checked, and the practice boxes were tested on an iPhone simulator and from the keyboard. Automated tools cannot judge everything; a listening test with a screen reader is part of the next review.

One limit from the platform

The pop-up definitions that LibreTexts adds to glossary words open with a mouse or a touch only. The same definitions are on the Glossary page at the end of the book, which works with the keyboard and with screen readers.

If you find a barrier

If you find a figure without a description, a control you cannot reach from the keyboard, or a video without accurate captions, tell your instructor, or report it at github.com/ItsDrDan/CHEM3Atextbook/issues, so it can be fixed in the book rather than worked around. Students who need materials in another format can also contact Fresno City College's Disabled Students Programs and Services (DSPS).

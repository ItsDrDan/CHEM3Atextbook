This page is for instructors who teach from this book or are deciding whether to. It explains how the book lines up with the CHEM 3A course outline, what each chapter contains, how the homework and practice tools work, and how to adapt the book for your own section.

Who the Book Is For

The book is written for CHEM 3A, Introductory General Chemistry, at Fresno City College (4 units: 3 lecture and 1 lab; C-ID CHEM 101). It follows the course outline of record (COR) that takes effect in Spring 2027, and it does not add topics beyond the outline or leave any out. About three quarters of CHEM 3A students are headed for allied health careers (nursing, dental hygiene, radiologic technology, respiratory therapy); the rest are biology, chemistry, and engineering students, many from the Central Valley. The examples and boxes are chosen for that audience.

What Each Chapter Contains

Every chapter follows the same pattern, so students learn where to look:

  • Section pages open with Learning Objectives and teach through worked examples in a Problem / Solution / Check format (about 440 in the book), Professor Tips, Common Mistakes, a Summary, and Exercises.
  • Every exercise and practice problem (1,398 in all) has a Hint that points to the first step and an Answer that shows the reasoning, not just the result.
  • Try It boxes (about 290) are short auto-graded practice sets inside the sections. Every answer choice, right or wrong, explains why.
  • Explore It boxes embed interactive labs and PhET simulations next to the skill they practice.
  • Video lessons are short ItsDrDan lectures, with links that jump to the key moments.
  • Connection boxes tie the chemistry to careers and to the region: Clinical Connections (nursing, dental hygiene, radiologic technology, respiratory therapy), Biology Connections, Valley Connections with cited local sources, and short Major Connections for science and engineering students. A running patient case, Mateo Herrera, an irrigation technician with asthma, appears only in chapters where the chemistry ties directly to his care, for example his oxygen therapy (gases), IV fluids (solutions), blood gases (acids and bases), and a lung scan (nuclear medicine).
  • The Chapter Guide collects the chapter on one page: Learning Objectives, Key Ideas, Key Equations, Key Terms, the video lessons, extra worked examples, and Common Mistakes.
  • Practice Problems (40 to 60 per chapter, every answer with its reasoning) and a Practice Games page that links the chapter's study games.

Main Path and Going Further

Everything in a chapter's numbered sections is the main path: what CHEM 3A students are expected to know. A few topics that go past the COR are kept on clearly labeled Going Further pages, so you can assign them to strong students or to a section that moves faster, without changing what the rest of the class is tested on:

  • 6.11 Going Further: resonance and exceptions to the octet rule.
  • 7.12 Going Further: balancing redox reactions in acidic and basic solution, and batteries.
  • 13.8 Going Further: Ka, Kb, Ksp, and the common-ion effect.
  • The phase-diagram part of 9.5, marked "Seen, Not Tested."

Some topics are taught lightly on purpose: the activity series (a short given list), gas density and molar mass (one paragraph and one example), and buffers (qualitative, with no Henderson–Hasselbalch equation). Equilibrium centers on Le Chatelier's principle, with K expressions and no ICE tables, and colligative-property problems usually stop at ΔT.

Homework and Practice Tools

ADAPT homework

Each chapter has an ADAPT homework assignment on the LibreTexts ADAPT platform: 50 questions drawn at random from a larger chapter pool, 2 points each (100 points), 3 attempts, and a Show Hint button with no penalty. Every question gives feedback on the correct answer and on each wrong choice. All of the questions are public and licensed CC BY 4.0 (author Daniel Kwasniewski), so you can find them in ADAPT's question bank, add them to your own assignments, or change the settings for your course.

In the book

  • Try It boxes give quick, ungraded checks inside each section; they need no login and work on a phone.
  • Practice Problems pages work well as problem sets or exam review, since every answer shows the reasoning.
  • Practice Games pages link the ItsDrDan study games for the chapter, including the Final Boss exam-review game, which opens on that chapter's topics and works well for cumulative review before the required ACS final.
  • Explore It boxes can be assigned as short in-class or at-home activities; several match the lab experiments (for example, the Build a Model lab in 6.3 is Experiment 6a on screen).

Course Conventions Used Throughout

  • Values come from the course reference sheets: the yellow sheet (periodic table, conversions, constants; Avogadro's number 6.0221 × 1023) and the green sheet (polyatomic ions, solubility rules, strong acids and bases).
  • Significant figures: trailing zeros without a decimal point are not significant (2500 has 2); a trailing decimal point makes them significant (2500. has 4).
  • Calculator tips are written for the TI-36X Pro.
  • The lab uses red cabbage indicator rather than a pH meter, and the book's lab connections follow the CHEM 3A lab manual.

Accessibility and Printing

Learning Objectives

  • Use a short activity series to predict whether a single-displacement reaction occurs.
  • Predict which metals react with acids to release hydrogen gas.

Zinc dropped into a copper(II) sulfate solution becomes coated with copper (Section 7.7). But a copper wire dropped into zinc sulfate solution just sits there. Some metals give up electrons more easily than others, and an activity series ranks them.

Reading the Activity Series

The rule is simple: a free metal displaces the ions of any metal below it in the series. The metal higher on the list is oxidized; the ion of the metal lower on the list is reduced. If the free metal is below the metal in the ion, there is no reaction.

  • Metals above hydrogen react with acids such as HCl(aq) to release H2 gas: Fe(s) + 2 HCl(aq) → FeCl2(aq) + H2(g).
  • Metals below hydrogen (copper, silver, gold) do not react with these acids, one reason they are used for jewelry, coins, and dental work. Gold sits so low that it barely corrodes at all (so does platinum, which is not on this short list).
  • The most active metals at the top (Li, K, Ca, Na) even react with water.
  • A more active metal can protect iron from rusting. Steel fencing and pipe are galvanized (coated with zinc), and boats carry blocks of magnesium or aluminum (a sacrificial anode). Zn, Mg, and Al are above Fe, so they are oxidized in place of the iron.

A reaction that works in one direction does not work in reverse. Zn + Cu2+ happens on its own, the way water runs downhill, so Cu + Zn2+ does not; the water does not run back uphill by itself. In the same way, a phone battery discharges on its own but needs a charger to go the other way.

Example \(\PageIndex{1}\): Will it react?

Problem. Predict whether each reaction occurs, and complete and balance those that do: (a) Mg(s) + Pb(NO3)2(aq); (b) Ag(s) + HCl(aq); (c) Ni(s) + CuCl2(aq); (d) Cu(s) + FeSO4(aq).

Solution.

  • (a) Mg is above Pb: Mg(s) + Pb(NO3)2(aq) → Mg(NO3)2(aq) + Pb(s).
  • (b) Ag is below H: no reaction.
  • (c) Ni is above Cu: Ni(s) + CuCl2(aq) → NiCl2(aq) + Cu(s).
  • (d) Cu is below Fe: no reaction.

Check. In every reaction that occurs, the metal that starts as an element is higher on the list than the metal that starts as an ion.

Professor Tip

Find both metals on the list before writing anything. Ask, "Is the lone metal higher than the metal in the compound?" Yes: write the swap and balance. No: write "no reaction" and move on. In CHEM 3A you will always be given the activity series; you do not need to memorize its order.

In the Kitchen: Cleaning Tarnished Silver

Silver tarnish is black silver sulfide, Ag2S (Section 7.7). Line a glass bowl with aluminum foil, add hot water with a spoonful of salt or baking soda, and set the tarnished silver on the foil so the two metals touch. The tarnish fades within minutes: aluminum is above silver, so aluminum is oxidized and the Ag+ in the tarnish is reduced back to silver metal. Use glass, not a metal bowl, so that only the foil takes part.

Common Mistakes

  • Comparing the wrong pair. Compare the free metal with the metal in the compound, not with the anion.
  • Writing products for "no reaction" cases.
  • Forgetting hydrogen is a diatomic gas: metal + acid gives H2(g).

Summary

  • A metal displaces ions of metals below it in the activity series; otherwise, no reaction.
  • Metals above H react with acids to release H2; Cu, Ag, and Au do not.
  • If a reaction occurs, its reverse does not happen on its own. A more active metal (Zn, Mg, Al) can protect iron by being oxidized first.

Exercises

Exercise \(\PageIndex{1}\)

Will Zn(s) react with HCl(aq)? If so, write the balanced equation.

Hint

Is zinc above or below H2 in the activity series? If above, it displaces hydrogen from the acid.

Answer

Yes (Zn is above H): Zn(s) + 2 HCl(aq) → ZnCl2(aq) + H2(g).

Exercise \(\PageIndex{2}\)

Which metal could be used to recover silver from a solution of AgNO3: Au or Cu? Write the equation.

Hint

A free metal displaces the ions of any metal below it. Which of the two metals is above silver?

Answer

Cu (above Ag): Cu(s) + 2 AgNO3(aq) → Cu(NO3)2(aq) + 2 Ag(s). Gold is below silver and would not react.

Exercise \(\PageIndex{3}\)

Does Sn(s) react with Al(NO3)3(aq)?

Hint

Find tin and aluminum in the activity series. Can a free metal displace the ions of a metal above it?

Answer

No: tin is below aluminum in the series.

A free metal displaces only the ions of a metal below it, and aluminum is above tin, so Sn cannot displace Al3+.

Exercise \(\PageIndex{4}\)

Predict the products and balance, or write no reaction: (a) Al(s) + FeCl2(aq); (b) Ag(s) + H2SO4(aq); (c) Mg(s) + Zn(NO3)2(aq).

Hint

Compare the free metal with the metal ion or H2 in the compound. If the free metal is higher, it reacts; balance chloride and nitrate as units.

Answer

(a) Al is above Fe: 2 Al(s) + 3 FeCl2(aq) → 2 AlCl3(aq) + 3 Fe(s) (Cl: 6 = 6). (b) Ag is below H: no reaction. (c) Mg is above Zn: Mg(s) + Zn(NO3)2(aq) → Mg(NO3)2(aq) + Zn(s).

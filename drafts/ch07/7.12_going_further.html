Balancing Redox in Acidic Solution

Many redox reactions in water involve oxygen-containing ions such as permanganate (MnO4−) or dichromate (Cr2O72−). Balancing them needs H2O and H+ as well as electrons. For each half-reaction:

  1. Balance all atoms except O and H.
  2. Balance O by adding H2O.
  3. Balance H by adding H+.
  4. Balance charge by adding electrons.

Then multiply the half-reactions so the electrons match, add them, and cancel anything that appears on both sides.

Example \(\PageIndex{1}\): Permanganate and iron(II)

Problem. Balance MnO4− + Fe2+ → Mn2+ + Fe3+ in acidic solution.

Solution. Reduction: MnO4− → Mn2+; add 4 H2O on the right for O; add 8 H+ on the left for H; charge on the left is −1 + 8 = +7, on the right +2, so add 5 e− on the left: MnO4− + 8 H+ + 5 e− → Mn2+ + 4 H2O. Oxidation: Fe2+ → Fe3+ + e−, times 5. Add:

MnO4− + 8 H+ + 5 Fe2+ → Mn2+ + 5 Fe3+ + 4 H2O

Check. Charge: −1 + 8 + 10 = +17 on the left; +2 + 15 = +17 on the right.

Basic Solution

Balance as if acidic, then add one OH− to both sides for each H+. Each H+ + OH− becomes H2O; cancel waters that appear on both sides.

Batteries

In a battery (a galvanic or voltaic cell), the oxidation and reduction half-reactions happen in separate places, so the electrons must travel through an outside wire, powering a device on the way. The electrode where oxidation happens is the anode; reduction happens at the cathode. A classic example separates the Zn and Cu2+ reaction of Section 7.7 into two beakers joined by a wire and a salt bridge; it produces about 1.1 volts. Alkaline batteries oxidize zinc and reduce manganese dioxide; lithium-ion batteries move lithium ions between electrodes as they charge and discharge. Pacemakers often use lithium–iodine batteries, chosen because they last for years inside the body.

Summary

  • Acidic solution: balance atoms (not O, H), then O with H2O, H with H+, charge with e−; match electrons and add.
  • Basic solution: add OH− to both sides to cancel H+.
  • Batteries separate oxidation (anode) and reduction (cathode) so electrons flow through a wire.

Exercises

Exercise \(\PageIndex{1}\)

Balance in acidic solution: Cu(s) + NO3−(aq) → Cu2+(aq) + NO(g).

Hint

Write the two half-reactions. For the nitrate half, balance N, then O with H2O, H with H+, and charge with electrons. Then match the electrons.

Answer

Oxidation: Cu → Cu2+ + 2 e− (×3). Reduction: NO3− + 4 H+ + 3 e− → NO + 2 H2O (×2). Net: 3 Cu + 2 NO3− + 8 H+ → 3 Cu2+ + 2 NO + 4 H2O.

Exercise \(\PageIndex{2}\)

In a zinc–copper cell, which electrode is the anode?

Hint

The anode is where oxidation happens. Which metal is higher in the activity series and so loses electrons?

Answer

The zinc electrode: zinc is oxidized there (Zn → Zn2+ + 2 e−).

# CHEM 3A – Introductory General Chemistry (Fresno City College) – Course Outline of Record

Source: `~/Downloads/CHEm 3a Course Outline Report.pdf` (CurriQunet report printed 10/30/26). Transcribed in full below; numbering is the COR's own.
Term effective **Spring 2027**. CC approval 03/23/2026; Board of Trustees 06/09/2026. 4 units (3 lecture + 1 lab), 18 weeks. C-ID CHEM 101. Prerequisite MATH 3A or MATH 103 (MATH 103 recommended for allied health and nursing). Replaces Chemistry 2A and 2B.
An older workgroup draft (Aug 2025) is at `~/Desktop/FCC Professional (Tenure, Committees, HR)/Committee Work/3A Work Group/CHEM-3A-Course Outline Report.docx`; the PDF above is the approved version and wins.

Description: Chemistry for applied science and non-science majors. The scientific method; chemical computations; composition of matter, energy, and physical and chemical changes; fundamental laws and principles; atomic and molecular structure; bonding; inorganic nomenclature, kinetic molecular theory, gas laws, solutions, acid-base theories, oxidation-reduction, equilibrium, nuclear chemistry, and qualitative and quantitative theories and techniques.

Listed OER text: *Introductory Chemistry, Atoms First for FCC* (Seth Yates, LibreTexts, 2025). "The latest edition or other suitable OER texts selected by the teaching faculty and approved by the Chemistry Department may be substituted."
Evaluation: final exam is the **ACS exam (required)**, at least 15% of grade; lab practical (lab skills stations rotation) required; minimum 10 hands-on experiments; students must pass lab to pass the course.

## Course objectives
1. Identify and apply the steps of the scientific method.
2. Use and estimate the magnitude of units in the metric system.
3. Perform chemical computations and apply dimensional analysis.
4. Use and interpret the periodic table.
5. Apply nomenclature to naming and writing of chemical formulas of common ions and compounds.
6. Classify elements, common ions, and compounds given the name or the chemical formula.
7. Classify and construct balanced chemical reaction equations.
8. Use fundamental laws, principles, and theories to predict and evaluate the structure and properties of elements, compounds, and ions.
9. Apply dynamic equilibrium concepts to evaluate the equilibrium distribution of reactants and products in chemical equilibrium reactions and perform equilibrium calculations.
10. Use atomic theory and radioactivity concepts to evaluate properties of elements and their isotopes.
11. Operate safely in a laboratory environment and use basic equipment to make measurements and collect data for processing.

## Student learning outcomes
1. Apply appropriate theories and techniques to solve quantitative problems.
2. Apply appropriate theories and techniques to solve qualitative problems.
3. Collect, record, organize, and analyze experimental data, and recognize the limitations of measurements.

## Lecture content

### COR 1 Matter: Atoms and Elements
1.1 Scientific Method
1.2 Matter: The Substance of Chemistry
- 1.2.1 Qualitative description of matter
  - Pure substances: elements and compounds (atoms, molecules and formula units)
  - Mixtures: homogeneous and heterogeneous
- 1.2.2 Understanding how electrostatic charges attract and repel
- 1.2.3 Distinguishing physical and chemical properties and changes
  - Physical properties: mass, color, melting point, boiling point, density (using density as a conversion factor)

1.3 Composition of the Atom
- 1.3.1 Historical roots of chemistry
- 1.3.2 Nuclear model of the atom: protons and the elements; electrons and element ions; neutrons and isotopes
- 1.3.3 The Bohr atomic model: electromagnetic energy spectrum and conceptual relation to atomic emission spectra
- 1.3.4 Calculation of the average atomic mass of an element

1.4 Quantum Mechanical Atomic Model
- 1.4.1 Shells, subshells, and electron distribution in orbitals: Aufbau fill order (electron distribution diagrams); Pauli exclusion principle (electron orbital diagrams)
- 1.4.2 Writing electron configurations of atoms or ions of main-group elements; valence electrons

1.5 Periodic Properties of the Elements
- 1.5.1 Overview of the periodic table by group
- 1.5.2 Octet rule and the formation of ions
- 1.5.3 Periodic trends: atomic radii, ionization energy

### COR 2 Nomenclature
2.1 Classifying chemical compounds
- Ionic compounds: predicting formula of binary ionic compounds and compounds with polyatomic ions; deducing ion charges from empirical formula
- Molecular (covalent) compounds
- Acids

2.2 Chemical nomenclature and formulas
- Naming compounds based on chemical formula and deducing formulae from names
- Ionic compounds with: metals with only one oxidation state possible; metals with more than one oxidation state possible; polyatomic ions
- Binary covalent nonmetal compounds
- Acids (binary and oxyacids), including acetic acid and oxalic acid

### COR 3 Measurement
3.1 Qualitative versus quantitative observations
3.2 Accuracy and precision
3.3 Significant figures: counting; rounding; uncertainty of measurements; calculations (addition/subtraction, multiplication/division, combined operations)
3.4 Scientific notation: calculations; significant figures of numbers in scientific notation
3.5 Units of measurement: metric system and SI units; SI unit prefixes
3.6 The mole as a unit of amount: relation to mass
3.7 Dimensional analysis

### COR 4 Chemical Composition and Bonding
4.1 Ionic bonding and ionic compounds: salts, bases, and oxides
4.2 Covalent bonds and molecular compounds
- Predicting number of bonds formed (single, double, triple)
- Predicting bond polarity using electronegativity
4.3 Lewis structure diagrams of molecules
- Molecules with one central atom, or simple organic molecules, **without minimizing formal charge**
- Predicting and naming molecular shape (VSEPR) of molecules with **no octet-rule exceptions, up to four electron groups**
4.4 Percent by mass of a compound
- Determination of the empirical formula of a compound
- Determination of the molecular formula of a compound
- Molecular mass and formula mass

### COR 5 Chemical Reactions
5.1 Signs of a chemical change
5.2 The meaning of a chemical reaction equation (conservation of mass)
- Writing chemical reaction equations: phase indicators
- Principles for balancing chemical equations
- Molecular, complete ionic, and net ionic equations
5.3 Classifying chemical reactions
5.4 Classifying by pattern of chemical change: double displacement (metathesis); single displacement; decomposition; combination
5.5 Classifying by characteristics: acid/base neutralization; precipitation; gas evolution; oxidation/reduction (redox), including combustion
- Assigning oxidation numbers
- Determine number of electrons transferred in a redox reaction, and write simple half-reactions for single-displacement reactions
- Determine oxidized and reduced species
- Determine oxidizing and reducing agents
5.6 Predicting products of chemical reactions
- Apply solubility rules for ionic compounds
- Recognize when unstable ion-exchange products form and gaseous products result
- Recognize when no reaction will occur: double displacement yielding all aqueous products; **comparative activity among the chemical elements (Activity Series)**

### COR 6 Stoichiometry
6.1 Introduction to fundamental mole concepts: using chemical formulas as conversion factors
6.2 Conversion factors to get to moles: Avogadro's number; molar mass; molarity (concentration); gases, including gases at STP
6.3 Stoichiometry: introduction to the reaction mole ratio
- Limiting reactant calculations
- Percent yield calculations

### COR 7 Energy and States of Matter
7.1 Overview of states of matter
7.2 Energy and chemistry
- Conservation of energy (macroscopic and microscopic)
- Energy and its forms: potential energy (macroscopic, chemical); kinetic energy (temperature; interconverting Fahrenheit, Celsius, Kelvin)
- Units of energy (calorie and joule)
- Calculating the heat of temperature changes: specific heat capacity
- Calculating the heat of phase changes
7.3 Properties of pure solids and liquids
- Unique properties of the condensed states
- Intermolecular forces in pure substances: London dispersion; dipole–dipole (molecular polarity); hydrogen bonding
- Crystalline solids: ionic solids; covalent solids
- Vapor pressure: relationship to boiling point; sublimation
- Ranking compounds by relative melting point or boiling point

### COR 8 Gases
8.1 Overview of kinetic molecular theory of gases
8.2 Units of pressure (mmHg, torr, atm)
8.3 Empirical gas law relations: Boyle's; Charles's; Avogadro's; combined gas law
8.4 Ideal gas law
- Gas-phase stoichiometry calculations
- **Gas density and molar mass**
- Dalton's law of partial pressures

### COR 9 Aqueous Systems and Solutions
9.1 Units of solution concentration: molarity; applications of molarity (the primary unit of solution concentration)
9.2 Preparing solutions to specific concentrations by dilution
9.3 Determination of the percent by mass of a solid
9.4 Titration methods: concentration of a liquid unknown using a liquid solution of known concentration; using a solid known
9.5 Properties of solutions
- Unique properties of homogeneous mixtures: effect of temperature on solubility of solids and gases; effect of partial pressure on gas solubility (Henry's law)
- Effect of intermolecular forces on solubility: hydrogen bonding; polar solvents and electrolyte solutes; solubility of polar and nonpolar solutes in polar solvents
- Colligative properties: boiling point elevation; freezing point depression; osmotic pressure

### COR 10 Acids, Bases and Salts
10.1 Definitions: Arrhenius; Brønsted–Lowry
10.2 Identifying acids, bases, and salts from chemical formula
10.3 Equilibrium dissociation of water: relationship between [H₃O⁺] and [OH⁻] in solutions of acids and bases
10.4 Determination of pH, pOH, [H₃O⁺] and [OH⁻] of **strong** acid/base solutions; definitions of acidic and basic solutions

### COR 11 Chemical Equilibrium
11.1 Qualitative overview of collision theory: effect of concentration and temperature on reaction rates
11.2 Introduction to dynamic equilibrium
11.3 Equilibrium constants and writing equilibrium expressions; qualitative interpretation
11.4 Effect of additions of reactants or products to a system at equilibrium: Le Chatelier's principle

### COR 12 Radioactivity and Nuclear Chemistry
12.1 Types of radioactivity: alpha, beta, gamma decay; relative penetrating and ionizing power
12.2 Natural radioactivity and half-life: calculations involving **whole numbers of half-lives**
12.3 Applications and examples: radiocarbon dating; nuclear fission (atomic bomb, nuclear power); nuclear fusion (the Sun); radioactivity in medicine

## Lab content
1. Laboratory orientation and safety
2. Scientific measurements using laboratory equipment and glassware (analytical balance, buret, pipet, graduated cylinder; accuracy and precision); chemical calculations, significant digits, metric system, scientific notation, unit analysis
3. Survey of chemical reactions by type and prediction of products (precipitation, gas evolution, acid–base, redox)
4. Determination of an empirical formula (e.g., magnesium oxide, a copper sulfide)
5. Stoichiometry; synthesis of a compound (e.g., alum, calcium phosphate)
6. Unknown concentration by titration and/or calibration curve (e.g., citric acid titration, vinegar)
7. Gas laws (e.g., collecting hydrogen over water, determining R)
8. Acids and bases (e.g., pH of household chemicals)
9. Thermochemistry (e.g., calorimetry)
10. Atomic structure (e.g., atomic emission spectra)

## Scope notes and conflicts

**Resolved 2026-11-14 by Dr. Dan:** activity series and gas density are both taught very lightly; enthalpy of reaction, qualitative buffers and molality (tied to osmolality) are in the main path even though the COR text does not name them, because FCC teaches them; equilibrium centers on Le Chatelier. See "Scope decisions" in `CLAUDE.md`. The original conflict table is kept below for the record.

| COR item | CLAUDE.md note | Conflict |
|---|---|---|
| 5.6 "Recognize when no reaction will occur … comparative activity among the chemical elements (Activity Series)" | "The activity series is CHEM 1A only." | The COR lists it under predicting products. |
| 8.4 "Gas density and molar mass" (under ideal gas law) | "No gas-density formula problems (those are CHEM 1A)." | The COR lists it. A middle path: treat density as mass/volume with PV = nRT (no separate d = PM/RT formula). |

Notes that agree with the COR (keep): quantum limited to shells/subshells/orbital diagrams/configurations (the COR names no quantum numbers ℓ, mℓ, ms); simple trends (atomic radius, ionization energy only; electronegativity appears in 4.2 for bond polarity); Lewis structures without formal-charge minimization; VSEPR up to four electron groups, no octet exceptions; pH only for strong acids/bases; half-life only in whole numbers of half-lives; equilibrium is expressions + qualitative + Le Chatelier (objective 9 says "perform equilibrium calculations"; keep these to writing K and simple plug-in, no ICE tables unless Dr. Dan says otherwise).

Wavelength/frequency/energy calculations: the COR says only "electromagnetic energy spectrum and conceptual relation to atomic emission spectra" (1.3.3). CLAUDE.md allows "simple wavelength/frequency/energy calculations"; that is slightly above the COR text but is standard and short. Flagged, not blocking.
